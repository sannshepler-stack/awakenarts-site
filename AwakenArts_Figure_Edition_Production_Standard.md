# AwakenArts Figure Edition Production Standard

**Status: Governing production standard for all Figure Editions.** Companion to `AwakenArts_Editorial_Philosophy.md`, which states why this specification exists — read that one first. This document governs mechanics: page sequence, language, visual rhythm, colophon. Where the two seem to conflict, the Philosophy document wins.

**How to read this document:** for every page below, the reader's experience is described before the page's construction — the governing question is *what should the reader experience here*, not *what belongs on this page*. Every rule below is the current working answer, sound until a future edition's own symbolic needs call for something else; that's a legitimate editorial choice, not a deviation to correct.

Every Figure Edition should be complete enough to remain useful long after its first reading, and to invite the reader back rather than simply be finished with.

**The Figure Edition Series Statement** governs every production decision below: *"Each Figure Edition invites readers into a symbolic story that becomes a way of recognizing the patterns that shape ordinary life."* Defined in full in `AwakenArts_Editorial_Philosophy.md` ("The Figure Edition Series Statement"). It defines the series, not any one edition, and every page-sequence, language, and visual-rhythm decision in this document exists in service of it. Use it, not ingredients-first language, on the website, in edition descriptions, and in any workshop or marketing material.

**Reference implementation — Dragon Figure Edition 11 (Founding Edition / Editorial Master).** Dragon (`AwakenArts_Workbook/02_Dragon/Figure_Edition/Dragon_Figure_Edition_11_Series_Prototype.pdf`, content in the companion Good Copy docx) was the prototype while these standards were still being discovered; now that the standards are codified in this document, Dragon is the series' founding reference rather than merely a prototype — the terminology reflects that the work has matured, not a change in function. Page rhythm, typography, transitions, image hierarchy, and white space remain under active review there. What has matured enough to be locked as series standard: the Encounter-through-Living-the-Message progression, recognition language over instructional language, Reflection followed by open journaling, Message Delivered as emotional culmination, and Living the Message as the bridge into ordinary life — all five are already reflected in the sections below. It supplies the working example; this document supplies the rule.

## 0. Editorial Workflow — Section-First Revision Policy *(governing directive, 2026-07-07, effective for all Figure Editions until otherwise instructed)*

**The principle.** During editorial review, words are the product. Production files are simply containers for those words. Do not rebuild production assets while the words themselves remain under review.

**Working Draft Phase.** Revise only the section currently under review. Present the revised text for approval as text, in chat. Do not update the master PDF, the production contact sheet, the collection contact sheet, the master DOCX, implementation logs, review documents, revision history, or any other production asset. Every revision during this phase is working copy only — assume a section will go through multiple refinements before approval, and do not treat any single revised paragraph as authorization to rebuild production files.

**Approval Phase.** A section's text is not final until it has been explicitly approved.

**Production Phase.** Production files are updated only after the current review cycle has concluded, or when specifically instructed. This includes the master DOCX, master PDF, production contact sheet, collection contact sheet, implementation log, and review documentation — all of them, together, not piecemeal.

**The governing command.** Wait for explicit instruction before touching any production file: **"Commit this section to production"** (for one approved section) or **"Commit all approved sections to production"** (once every section in the current review cycle is closed). Only those instructions trigger rebuilding the DOCX, PDF, contact sheets, logs, and review documents — approving a section's wording is not, on its own, that instruction.

**Working sequence this governs, as an example (not a fixed order):** revise a section, approve it; revise the next section, approve it; repeat until every section in the cycle is closed; then commit all approved sections to production in one pass.

## 1. The Figure Edition and the Figure Companion

Two separate publications, two separate audiences. See `AwakenArts_Editorial_Philosophy.md`, "Two Publications: Edition and Companion," for the reasoning; this section states the resulting production requirement. ("Figure Companion" supersedes the earlier term "Leader's Resource (Facilitator Guide)" as of 2026-07-07 — facilitator guidance is one thing the Companion provides, not the whole of what it is.)

| | [Figure] Edition | [Figure] Companion |
|---|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Symbolic encounter, personal recognition, reflection, journaling, continued awareness | To deepen understanding of the Edition without interrupting its literary integrity |
| **Contains** | Cover, atmosphere, figure, poem, recognition, reflection, journaling, closing movement, colophon | Origin material, interpretive essay, extended reflection, discussion objectives, group questions, teaching cautions, workshop timing, facilitator scripts, safety notes |
| **Never contains** | Facilitation scripts, timing charts, teaching cautions, leader instructions | — |
| **Stands alone?** | Yes — complete enough for an individual or an unassisted small group | Yes — but presumes the Edition as its companion text |

**Production rule:** facilitator-specific material — scripts, timing, teaching cautions, sequencing guidance — is removed from the Edition entirely for any figure that has a Companion. It is not summarized, condensed, or partially retained there. This is a hard boundary, not a matter of degree.

## 1a. The Figure Companion — Internal Architecture (series standard, 2026-07-07)

Discovered during the Dragon Companion recovery process and approved as the permanent architecture governing every future Figure Companion (Grismere, Bowls, Queen Ann, Poppy, and beyond). This is the reader's architecture, not merely a documentary one — it describes four distinct editorial functions a Companion performs, in this order:

| Layer | Editorial function | Dragon's worked example |
|---|---|---|
| **Foundation** | Preserve the author's first encounter with the figure. Provides a brief glimpse into how the figure emerged before later literary, psychological, and theological development. Establishes the author's relationship with the figure rather than explaining the figure itself. | Author's Perspective (from Author Notes) |
| **Interpretation** | What the figure reveals — the Companion's interpretive center. All other Companion sections either prepare for it, support it, or apply it. | Learning |
| **Engagement** | How a reader encounters the figure — two parallel sections, neither absorbed into the other | Reflection (individual) and Facilitator Notes (group) |
| **Application** | Demonstrates how the Companion is implemented in practice | Workshop Plan (its first expression; future figures may also have retreats, study groups, online courses, or sermons here) |

Governing notes:

- **Foundation is preserved, not externalized.** The author's original voice — before editorial development — has independent value and belongs inside the Companion in some form, even if abbreviated. It is not folded into Interpretation, and it is not pushed out to an external archive.
- **Interpretation is singular and central.** A Companion has one Interpretation section. Everything else in the Companion refers back to it rather than restating it, and every other section relates to it as preparation, support, or application.
- **Engagement is parallel, not sequential-only.** Individual (Reflection) and group (Facilitator Notes) engagement are two separate, complete paths from the same Interpretation — neither is a subset of the other.
- **Application's publication placement is a layout decision, not an architectural one.** Whether a given figure's Application section becomes an appendix, a standalone facilitator resource, or a downloadable supplement is decided at layout time, per figure — the architecture only establishes that Application exists and that it demonstrates use rather than teaching.

This architecture describes editorial function and relationship only. It does not, by itself, fix chapter order, page count, or a table of contents for any given figure — those are determined separately, per figure, once the architecture is applied to that figure's actual recovered material.

### Author's Perspective — scope and standing workflow (series standard, 2026-07-07)

Every Figure Companion's Foundation section is called Author's Perspective. Its purpose is narrow and deliberately small: to let the reader glimpse how the symbolic figure first emerged and how the author's understanding of it deepened through continued reflection. It is not a memoir, a journal, or a production history, and its subject is not the author's life — it is the figure's unfolding recognition. One to three pages is normally sufficient.

**Standing workflow — a light habit, not a recovery project.** For Dragon, Author's Perspective was assembled after the fact, from a preexisting notes document, because that was the material available. Going forward, this is not the intended method. The material should be drawn naturally from the actual creative conversation as a figure develops, not reconstructed afterward. In practice: when Susan makes a statement in the course of discussing a figure — an early observation, a symbolic discovery, an image that wouldn't resolve — either she or Claude may simply note "this belongs in Author's Perspective," and it is set aside for that figure's Companion as it happens. No separate archival or recovery pass is required at the end.

## 2. Page Sequence — What the Reader Experiences, Then What Appears

Every Figure Edition follows the same movement (see the Reader Journey in the Philosophy document): Encounter → Notice → Recognize → Reflect → Respond → Return to Ordinary Life. The page sequence below is how that movement is currently built, and is the current working answer for how to build it — unless the symbolic needs of a future edition require another solution. Page count follows the sequence, not the reverse — a figure with greater depth may require more pages; none should be added or removed without the movement itself changing.

### Cover
**Reader experience:** first contact with the figure's name and its central tension, before any content begins.
**Page:** edition title; a short line stating the edition's thesis (Dragon: "Not This or That, But And."); AwakenArts mark; full-bleed cover image.

### Begin *(production term: Encounter Threshold)*
**Reader experience:** the reader is asked to slow down before meeting the figure. Mystery, not information.
**Page:** full-bleed atmospheric image; at most one short line of text inviting stillness. No section label naming what comes next.

### The Image *(production term: The Figure / The Motif)*
**Reader experience:** the reader meets the figure itself, contemplatively — held, not explained.
**Page:** figure artwork centered on a dark ground, cream mat, restrained border, full figure visible, never cropped. No instructional content on this page. A companion symbolic image (motif) may accompany or follow the figure, using the same framing, to expand the figure's symbolic world without analysis.

### The Poem
**Reader experience:** the reader receives the edition's literary center in the author's own words, without introduction or paraphrase.
**Page:** the complete poem, shaped or conventionally typeset as the individual work requires; the author's name and copyright may be embedded within the poem's own design as part of its presentation; a pull quote below, in white space, attributed to the author.

### Recognition *(structure closed as series standard, 2026-07-07 — see note below)*
**Reader experience:** the reader is invited to notice, then to see the pattern the figure embodies — arriving at insight through their own attention rather than being told what to conclude.
**Page(s):** begins with an invitation to observe both the figure and the reader's own response to it, before any analysis. Develops the recognition in the fewest words that do the work — the length and page count here are a production choice, not a fixed requirement. Closes by returning attention to the image with new eyes.

**The four-step movement (series standard).** Recognition's tenth revision on Dragon settled a structure Susan has closed as the model every future edition should emulate, not just Dragon's own solution:

1. **Observe** — see the image differently; name the condition it depicts without implying a cause or diagnosis.
2. **Explore the Pattern** — understand what the figure mistakes (the false opposition it takes for a battle).
3. **Develop the Insight** — recognize the same pattern in human experience, grounded in the image itself rather than in a therapeutic or psychological framework layered onto it.
4. **Return to the Image** — see the figure again with new eyes, closing on a sentence that does not answer the question it raises but creates the expectation that an answer exists ("received before it is lived" is Dragon's own working example, not a line to reuse verbatim).

The reader ends where they began — but seeing something they could not see before. **Governing standard, learned across ten revisions:** Recognition names a recognizable human condition and stops; it does not explain the figure, and it does not lean on interpretive or psychological language to do the naming for the reader. Each edition finds its own symbolic language and its own sentences — the four-step movement is the structure, not the wording. See `Dragon_Christian_Fulfillment_Review.md`, Page 5, for the revision history this standard was drawn from.

**Status: closed.** Per Susan's explicit direction, this section is not to be revisited for Dragon again unless a future edition surfaces something fundamentally new about the Recognition page itself — in which case the discovery is tested against a new edition first, the same way this standard itself was discovered on Dragon, before this note is amended.

Reader-facing language should invite recognition rather than explanation. Questions are preferred to conclusions whenever possible.

### The Larger Story *(title resolved, 2026-07-07 — retained permanently as the series standard; see "Page Titles and Reader Voice" note below)*
**What this page actually is.** Not a commentary on the symbolic figure, and not the symbolic figure's story restated in Christian language. It is a conversation among witnesses: the symbolic figure bears witness through image and story; Scripture bears witness through God's revealed Word; the Christian tradition bears witness through those who have reflected on these same truths throughout the history of the Church; the reader is then invited to recognize that same movement within ordinary life. Each voice speaks in its own right, alongside the others, rather than one voice explaining or standing in for another.

**What the symbolic figure is never treated as, on this page or anywhere else in the edition:** never the source of wisdom, never a parallel gospel, never a biblical character. It is a symbolic witness that prepares the reader to hear the Christian story with fresh eyes — real, necessary work, and a different job than the one Scripture and the Christian tradition do next. This page is not Christian content attached to an otherwise complete edition; the edition was never "complete" without it in the first place.

**The five-part movement this page exists to carry out, in order:** the symbolic story awakens recognition; Scripture reveals God's truth; the Christian tradition helps illuminate that truth; Christ remains the center; the reader is invited to live in that reality. The three voices on the page (below) carry the first three steps; "Returning to the Story" carries the fourth and hands off to the fifth — which is why its closing paragraph must pass the movement to the reader rather than ending on the figure or on Scripture alone (see voice 3, below, and "the final beat belongs to the reader").

**Reader experience:** the recognition just reached on the previous page is brought into contact with the Christian story — not proven, not appended, but shown to already be addressed within the biblical story. The reader moves from symbolic recognition to biblical recognition without the literary movement stopping to explain itself. Scripture is the center of gravity on this page, not a commentary on the symbolic figure: the reader should feel that the symbolic story prepared them to hear Scripture, not that the symbolic story is being explained by it. The symbolic figure never enacts or fulfills Scripture in its own right — it gives symbolic form to a human struggle that Scripture reveals within the larger story of God's redemptive work.

**Page:** required in every Figure Edition, placed immediately after Recognition and before Reflection. Built from three integrated voices, in order, each doing a distinct job (see Editorial Philosophy, "Sources of Interpretation"). The three section headings establish a recognizable rhythm reused across every edition — **Scripture / The Christian Tradition / Returning to the Story** — with the third heading personalized to the figure itself (e.g., "Returning to the Dragon"):

1. **Scripture (the authoritative witness)** — the biblical narrative that most naturally tells the same story as the edition's symbolic movement, not a verse selected for matching vocabulary. A short, direct, accurately cited quotation — long enough to carry the narrative's shape, short enough to stay literary rather than becoming a Bible-study excerpt. **Governing standard: the biblical passage is selected because it shares the same redemptive movement as the symbolic story, not merely similar vocabulary or themes.** This is the lesson recorded from Dragon's own revision history — the first draft (Ephesians 2:14) was selected for matching words and was corrected in favor of a passage selected for matching narrative movement. **A second, sharper standard, learned from a further correction: matching narrative shape is not sufficient on its own — the passage must also match the symbolic story's theological center of gravity.** Dragon's own history demonstrates why: Luke 8's Legion narrative (the passage the first correction above arrived at) rhymed with Dragon's plot closely enough to survive a full dedicated research pass, yet it tells a story of deliverance from an external, invading force — the wrong center of gravity for a story about one person's own opposing parts needing reconciliation, not exorcism. It was replaced with Romans 7:15 and 8:1–2, chosen by asking a sharper question than "which passage looks like the symbolic story": *what biblical truth makes the story's own reconciliation possible?* See `Dragon_Christian_Tradition_Research.md`, Editorial Objective 1, and `Dragon_Christian_Fulfillment_Review.md`, Page 6, sixth revision, for this correction in full.
2. **The Christian Tradition (the interpretive witness)** — one or two brief, accurately quoted passages from voices from the Christian tradition (Church Fathers, Reformers, theologians, spiritual writers, Christian philosophers and psychologists, contemporary pastors) who have described the same struggle or transformation. Their purpose is to witness that this movement has already been recognized across centuries, not to validate the edition or argue the point — the edition does not become Christian because a respected voice agrees with it. **Governing standard: voices from the Christian tradition should be selected because they illuminate the biblical witness rather than replace it. They serve Scripture, not the reverse.** Treat them as companions in an ongoing conversation, not as competing authorities — this section should never read as "Scripture plus quotations." A brief bridging line connecting the voices to each other and to Scripture (rather than presenting them as isolated pull-quotes) is part of doing this well; the reader should feel they are entering a conversation that has continued across centuries, not being shown supporting citations. Every quotation must be attributed by name and source, and cleared against that voice's or publisher's quotation-permission policy before an edition goes to print — unlike Scripture (public domain in translations such as the KJV), quotations from named contemporary or twentieth-century voices are under copyright. (Named "Christian Reflection" in earlier drafts; renamed because it reads like a devotional heading rather than telling the reader what is actually happening on the page — voices from the Church helping us think about the struggle just recognized.)
3. **Returning to the Story (the literary witness)** — a brief paragraph, in the edition's own voice, that returns the reader to the symbolic figure and lets Scripture, the Christian tradition, and the symbolic story illuminate one another — without ever implying the figure enacted or performed what Scripture reveals. Closes by naming what Christ has done and inviting personal response, not merely inviting the reader to notice a pattern: the closing movement is symbolic figure → recognition, Scripture → revelation, Christ → reconciliation, reader → response. **The final beat belongs to the reader, not to the figure.** Avoid closing on the figure taking further action (even a small, symbolic one) that parallels or restates what Scripture's own witness already did — the figure's part in this page is to prepare recognition, not to re-enact the passage's own outcome a second time. Let the last movement pass naturally to the reader's own life. Functions as the bridge into Reflection.

Each figure will lead to different Scripture, different voices from the tradition, and different themes — this page's content is edition-specific, but the movement, its three-voice structure, and its heading rhythm are not: symbolic recognition awakens the question, Scripture reveals, the Christian tradition helps the reader understand, and Christ remains the center. **There is no standing roster of voices reused across editions.** Which voices illuminate a given figure is discovered by researching that edition's own central struggle, not chosen from a fixed list — Grismere may call for Lewis and Nouwen again, or for entirely different voices (Madeleine L'Engle, Elisabeth Elliot, a Church Father); Bowls and Queen Ann will each find their own. Every edition develops its own conversation with the tradition, which is richer than requiring the series to sound alike. See Editorial Philosophy, "The Christian Story: Where Recognition Finds Its Fulfillment," and "Sources of Interpretation," for the governing rationale, `Dragon_Christian_Tradition_Research.md` for the research method behind selecting Scripture and tradition voices, and Section 11 below for the review discipline this page's content should pass before it is finalized.

**Title, resolved.** Per Susan's directive of 2026-07-07 ("Editorial Directive — Page Titles and Reader Voice"), after reviewing the completed Editorial Philosophy and the direction of Dragon's own revisions, "The Larger Story" is retained as the series-standard title, permanently — not revisited edition to edition. Deliberately not titled "The Christian Story," "The Gospel Story," or "Scripture's Answer": those titles announce the page's conclusion before the reader has entered it, which is exactly the posture the page itself works against. "The Larger Story" accomplishes what the page is for — gently inviting the reader beyond the symbolic story into the larger story of God's redemptive work, without feeling imposed or argumentative. Governing posture: invitation, not persuasion. Retain this title as the series standard unless a future edition presents a compelling reason to depart from it.

### Reflect *(retitled from "Reflection," 2026-07-07 — see "Page Titles and Reader Voice" note below; question structure closed as series standard, 2026-07-07 — see note below)*
**Reader experience:** the recognition turns personal — now informed by both the symbolic story and the Christian story, not the symbolism alone. The reader is invited to locate the pattern in their own life, privately, without any sense of being tested or graded.
**Page:** a small number of open prompts (three is the current working model), each moving from personal awareness toward a more integrative question. No numbering or lettering of the prompts — this is a personal encounter, not a worksheet. Permission-based instruction (see Section 6, Writing Invitations). The prompts themselves stay focused on the reader's own life, unchanged in form; a single introductory sentence may invite the reader to hold both the symbolic story and what The Larger Story just named, without turning the private prompts into a Bible study. **Prompts should ask, not interpret** — a prompt that tells the reader what they are feeling or where their struggle will resolve (e.g., naming it as "waiting to be reconciled" or a "longing for rest") oversteps; a prompt that only asks the reader to locate and describe the pattern in their own life does the page's actual job. See Dragon's own revision (`Dragon_Christian_Fulfillment_Review.md`) for the corrected working example.

**The three-question movement (series standard).** Dragon's finalized Reflect settled a question-by-question progression Susan named directly and asked to lock in as a path for future editions, not only Dragon's own three questions:

1. **Remember** — an open, ordinary-life question that asks the reader to recall a specific instance, without presuming what it means.
2. **Recognize** — a question, in the edition's own established vocabulary, that lets the reader discover whether an outward conflict also reveals an inward one — genuinely open; some conflicts will, some won't.
3. **Reimagine** — a question that asks what becomes possible once the pattern is seen differently, staying at the level of possibility rather than resolution. This question stops short of the reconciliation The Larger Story goes on to name — Reflect's restraint is what gives that page its proper place.

Each edition will ask its own three questions, in its own vocabulary and its own symbolic figure — the remember/recognize/reimagine movement is the structure, not the wording, the same way Recognition's four-step movement is a structure future editions fill with their own language. **Governing standard:** Reflect does not promise healing, integration, or reconciliation at any point in the progression; it invites honest engagement with the struggle and leaves the answer open for what follows.

**Page Titles and Reader Voice.** Susan's governing directive, 2026-07-07: "Reflection" described the page's content; "Reflect" invites participation, matching the existing imperative "Begin" and shifting the page from describing content to inviting it. No further page-title changes are recommended for Dragon at this time; the remaining titles already describe the reader's progression accurately and become the working standard for future Figure Editions as well.

**Page order — RESOLVED, 2026-07-07.** An early listing of the titles in this same directive placed Reflect ahead of The Larger Story, which did not match Dragon's built order (The Larger Story at page 6, Reflect at page 7). Put to Susan directly; she has since confirmed the built order is correct and closed the question: **Recognition → Reflect → The Larger Story → Living the Message.** Her reasoning, tied directly to the methodology this project established: Recognition names what is genuinely true; Reflect lets the reader remember, recognize, and reimagine; The Larger Story receives that recognition into the Christian story; Living the Message invites the reader to carry it into life in Christ. Reflect must precede The Larger Story because it is the recognition being received, not the reverse. No further discussion needed; this is settled as the series-standard sequence, not only Dragon's own.

### Journal
**Reader experience:** the reader is given uninterrupted space to stay with whichever prompt held them.
**Page:** one brief instruction directing the reader back to the prompt that stayed with them; ruled writing space to the bottom margin. The quietest page in the edition — no recurring visual ornament here, so the reader's own writing is the only thing on the page.

### Message Delivered
**Reader experience:** the edition's thesis is declared, plainly and briefly, as a kind of arrival — the emotional and symbolic climax of the edition.
**Page:** the Message Delivered page is the ceremonial center of every AwakenArts Figure Edition. Its composition remains substantially consistent throughout the series. The large AwakenArts S-shaped ampersand occupies the visual center of the page as the series' recurring symbolic mark. The edition's closing recognition changes; the page architecture does not. Deep navy background; one concise statement expressing the edition's central recognition; a closing signature statement unique to that edition.

The statement of central recognition should let the ampersand's presence on the page do its own work rather than explain what the symbol means — naming it ("the ampersand is not ornament but revelation...") over-explains what the page is already demonstrating visually (see Editorial Philosophy, Preserve Mystery). The symbol is evidence here, not the statement's subject.

### Living the Message *(the closing movement — see Editorial Philosophy, "Recognition, Not Interpretation")*
**Reader experience:** the reader is invited to carry the recognition past the last page, into ordinary life — not to complete an exercise, but to keep noticing. The purpose is not to produce answers but to cultivate continued noticing.
**Page:** a short passage stating that the encounter continues past the book, followed by two or three open, invitational questions oriented toward ordinary life, and a closing line naming that they work equally well alone or in conversation — the edition's only accommodation to small-group use, never expanding into facilitation guidance.

The closing questions are a standard in their own right, not Dragon-specific — every edition's closing movement should encourage continued recognition in ordinary life. One of the three is required verbatim in function, not just pattern; the others are adaptable:

- **The Bridge Principle (required in every edition):** every closing movement must bridge the symbolic encounter to ordinary life — see Editorial Philosophy, "The Bridge Principle." Dragon's working example: *Where have you begun to notice this pattern in ordinary life?* A future edition may build its own bridge in different words; what's required is the function, not this exact sentence — returning the reader to ordinary life without explaining what to find there.
- *Did you notice anything new?* and *How can seeing this differently influence what comes next?* — supporting questions; future editions may phrase their own versions rather than reusing these verbatim, so long as the register stays invitational (Editorial Voice, Section 3) and the purpose stays the same: cultivating continued noticing, not producing answers.

### About This Edition *(production term: Colophon)*
**Reader experience:** a quiet closing courtesy — who made this, and where to find more — without breaking the tone of what came before.
**Page:** see Section 7 below for required contents.

**Removed from the Participant Edition:** Facilitator Notes. See Section 1.

## 3. Reader-Centered Language

Every page title, heading, and instruction a reader actually encounters is reviewed against one test: does this answer the reader's implicit question, "What am I doing here?" — or does it describe the page's function within the production system? Production vocabulary is useful while building an edition; it does not belong on a page a reader holds.

Reader-facing language should invite recognition rather than explanation. Questions are preferred to conclusions whenever possible.

**Reader-facing title reference** (production term → reader-facing title):

| Production term | Reader-facing title |
|---|---|
| Encounter Threshold | Begin |
| The Figure / The Motif | The Image |
| The Poem | The Poem |
| Recognition I / Recognition II | Recognition |
| Where It Meets You | Reflect |
| Guided Journaling | Journal |
| Message Delivered | Message Delivered |
| *(closing movement)* | Living the Message *(or the equivalent name adopted for a given edition)* |
| Colophon | About This Edition |
| Facilitator Notes | *(removed — lives in the Leader's Resource only)* |

Evocative words that name an experience rather than a production category — Recognition, Reflection, Encounter as a series-wide term — are not jargon and do not need replacing. The test is function, not vocabulary length: a word stays if a reader would use it to describe what's happening to them; it goes if it describes how the page was built.

**Reduce instructional and educational language.** Prefer the language on the right wherever the language on the left would otherwise appear in reader-facing copy:

| Reduce | Prefer |
|---|---|
| Teaching | Noticing |
| Instruction | Recognition |
| Educational objectives | Discovery |
| Curriculum | Reflection |
| Learning outcomes | Response, Integration |
| — | New ways of seeing |

Editions should sound literary, not educational. This applies to reader-facing copy; it does not apply within the Leader's Resource, where instructional language is appropriate to its purpose.

### Editorial Voice

AwakenArts editions:

- invite rather than instruct
- observe rather than diagnose
- recognize rather than explain
- ask rather than persuade
- remain literary rather than academic

This is the touchstone to return to whenever new writing drifts — a line of copy, a page title, or a prompt that no longer sounds like this list describes it should be revised before it goes further.

## 4. Visual Rhythm

Visual continuity across every Figure Edition is carried by a consistent set of recurring elements, not by identical page layouts. A reader should feel the rhythm without consciously noticing it.

**Recurring elements:**
- **Color palette** — deep navy, terracotta/amber (poem-derived pull quotes only), gold (labels, rules, ornament), cream (content pages). Individual atmospheric images may introduce additional natural colors where the figure requires it.
- **Dark/light page cadence** — the edition should move through an emotional arc expressed as alternating dark and cream pages: encounter (mystery) → understanding (clarity) → reflection (intimacy) → declaration (ceremony) → resource (accessible). The arc is the standard; exact page-by-page color assignment is a working model that may vary by figure.
- **Typography** — a consistent hierarchy (section label, title, rule, pull quote, body, instructional framing, footer) applied identically across every edition; only the figure's own imagery and palette accents change.
- **Spacing** — generous, deliberate white space throughout. White space is a feature of the contemplative reading experience, not an unfinished layout — see Section 9.
- **Headers and footer system** — consistent placement and treatment across every page.
- **The AwakenArts ampersand** — the official AwakenArts S-shaped ampersand is the recurring editorial ornament across the entire Figure Edition series, not a symbol each edition discovers independently. Individual editions may introduce their own supporting symbolic motifs; the ampersand itself remains the fixed, consistent visual connector throughout the collection.

**Guidelines for the ampersand's use — contrast, not consistency of scale:**
- On the Message Delivered page: gold, positioned in the upper third of the page with generous white space beneath it before the text begins — present, not dominant. Its role is symbolic, not illustrative; sized to be recognized, not to fill the page.
- Everywhere else the ornament appears: small and quiet — an editorial ornament or section connector, never competing with the page's other content.
- Consistent placement and weight within each of those two registers; never used purely as decoration or to fill empty space.
- The text of the edition should never explain what the ampersand means (see the Message Delivered entry above) — let it work visually, as evidence of the recognition rather than its subject.

**Message Delivered visual hierarchy, in order:** (1) the page's purpose — arrival, culmination; (2) the ampersand, the series' symbolic mark; (3) the central recognition statement; (4) the closing signature line. The reader's eye should move through that sequence — symbol first, then reflection — which requires real separation between the ampersand and the text beneath it, not a tight visual pairing. The reader should leave remembering the message, not the size of the mark. As the series grows, this page becomes immediately recognizable as an AwakenArts edition — but what should linger after the page turns is the recognition, not the ornament. The ampersand's job is to reinforce that recognition, not compete with it.

## 5. Usability Standards

Whenever a later page asks the reader to reconsider the artwork already shown (most often within Recognition), a small reference reproduction of that artwork shall appear adjacent to the observation text. A reader should never need to flip back several pages simply to locate the primary image the text is asking them to reconsider. This is a permanent production standard, not a per-edition judgment call.

---

## 6. Writing Invitations

Whenever a reader is invited to write or reflect, the instruction shall offer permission rather than direction.

**Preferred pattern:** *"You may write in these pages, in your own journal, or simply spend a few quiet moments reflecting."*

**Avoid:** language that reads as an assignment, a requirement, or a classroom prompt ("complete the following," "answer each question," numbered/lettered prompts presented as steps).

The edition should feel companionable throughout — a quiet presence beside the reader's own process — rather than instructional.

---

## 7. Colophon ("About This Edition")

Standardized across every Figure Edition. Required:

- AwakenArts identity (wordmark, tagline)
- Publication year
- Copyright: "© [Year] Susan Ann Shepler. All rights reserved."
- Website reference: AwakenArts.com
- One-sentence author statement
- Collection identification (a brief line placing the edition within the AwakenArts collection)
- A reference to the edition's Leader's Resource, where one exists

Keep this page concise. The specific wording may be refined as the AwakenArts publishing identity matures, but the required elements above do not vary by edition.

---

## 8. Production Consistency

Every transition, heading, page turn, ornament, and recurring design element should be reviewed as part of a complete reading experience, not evaluated page by page in isolation. Page turns should feel intentional — each one either deepens the current movement or carries the reader into the next one deliberately, never abruptly. The emotional rhythm established across the edition (Section 4) should remain uninterrupted from cover to colophon, including at the boundary where a Leader's Resource's material used to sit — that boundary should now simply not exist within the Participant Edition, rather than being visible as a seam.

---

## 9. Contact Sheets

Every Figure Edition gets two contact sheets, built the same way for every edition so the series reads as one coherent body of work rather than separate experiments:

**Production Contact Sheet** — every page of the edition, laid out in a bare grid on a plain cream field: no border, no title bar, no added captions. Each page's own printed footer (number, section, title) is what identifies it — nothing is added on top of the actual page. Documents the complete edition; used internally, for development and review. Naming: `{Edition}_Figure_Edition_Production_Contact_Sheet.png`.

**Collection Contact Sheet** — six to eight representative pages only, same bare-grid style, larger thumbnails. Chosen for what best conveys the edition to someone who hasn't seen it: typically the cover, the atmospheric opening image, the central figure image, the poem, one recognition or reflection page, and the closing pages (Message Delivered, Living the Message, colophon). Working pages that are more workbook than image or statement (open reflection prompts, blank journaling pages) are generally left out. Used on the website and in marketing. Naming: `{Edition}_Figure_Edition_Collection_Contact_Sheet.png`.

The production sheet documents the edition; the collection sheet sells it. Neither one adds design chrome the printed pages don't already have — representativeness is the whole point.

**Uses**

The Production Contact Sheet serves as the archival and editorial record of the complete edition. It may also accompany the Participant Edition as a collector's reference, or be provided upon request to those wishing to review the complete publication before purchasing.

The Collection Contact Sheet is a publishing and marketing asset. It serves as the primary visual overview of a Figure Edition for websites, workshop descriptions, facilitator materials, speaking engagements, brochures, product pages, proposals, and participant information. Its purpose is to communicate the character and experience of the edition before purchase or participation — what the edition feels like, not what pages it contains.

---

## 9a. Internal Working Name vs. Public Name

Every Figure Edition carries two names, and the two are never mixed:

**Internal working name** — used in documentation, internal filenames, and editorial records only. Pattern: `{Figure} Figure Edition {version} — Founding Edition (Editorial Master)` once a version is locked as the edition's governing master (see Dragon: "Dragon Figure Edition 11 — Founding Edition (Editorial Master)"). Version numbers, revision history, and "Editorial Master"/"Founding Edition" language belong here and nowhere else.

**Public name** — everything the reader, buyer, or visitor sees: the cover, the website, marketing material. Exactly two lines, no more: the figure's name (e.g., "The Dragon"), and "An AwakenArts Figure Edition." No version number, no "Edition N," no internal designation of any kind. Readers don't need to know it's Edition 11 — that's publishing history, not their experience.

**Legacy text is not preserved by default.** If an existing cover or asset has internal naming baked into it (an edition number, a redundant repeated wordmark), the public-name standard overrides it going forward — the fact that text is already embedded in the artwork is not a reason to keep it once a cleaner standard exists. A figure-specific approved tagline (Dragon's "Not this or that, but and.") is content, not internal naming, and is preserved on its own merits.

---

## 10. Applying the Governing Editorial Test

`AwakenArts_Editorial_Philosophy.md` states the five-question editorial test governing every decision in this specification: does it deepen recognition; does it help the reader notice something previously unseen; does it preserve mystery rather than over-explain; does it encourage recognition in ordinary life; does it increase the usefulness of the edition. Any proposed change to this Production Standard, or to an individual edition built from it, should be checked against that test before adoption — including changes that look like straightforward production improvements (spacing, numbering, added pages). A production choice that fails the test should be reconsidered even if it is otherwise well executed.

**The dual standard, recorded 2026-07-06 (see Editorial Philosophy, "The Vocation of a Figure Edition"), applies before any edition is signed off as finished.** Every edition is judged on two independent levels, and both must pass:

1. Does the edition stand as a complete literary work on its own? Everything before The Larger Story — image, poem, Recognition, guided reflection — should possess full artistic integrity even for a reader who stops there.
2. Does The Larger Story faithfully receive that literary work into the truth revealed in Christ, per Editorial Philosophy's "Recognition and Truth"?

An edition that passes only one of these is not finished, no matter how well it satisfies the other. This check is separate from, and in addition to, the five-question test above and the Christian Fulfillment Lens in Section 11.

---

## 11. Applying the Christian Fulfillment Lens

Every existing and future Figure Edition is now also reviewed and developed through the lens set out in `AwakenArts_Editorial_Philosophy.md` ("The Christian Story: Where Recognition Finds Its Fulfillment"), from the earliest development stage rather than as a closing pass:

1. What human pattern is this edition revealing?
2. What truth is the reader being invited to recognize?
3. What aspect of the Gospel does this recognition prepare the reader to receive?
4. How does this movement participate in the larger Christian story?
5. Does the edition remain faithful both to the symbolic story and to the Christian understanding of transformation?

*Question 3 above is itself a correction, recorded 2026-07-06.* It previously read "Where does this recognition naturally resonate with Scripture?" — a question that, however carefully applied, still starts by hunting for a passage the symbolic story resembles. See "The purpose of The Larger Story is theological, not a Scripture search" below for why this was corrected and what governs the corrected version.

This runs alongside the Governing Editorial Test in Section 10, not in place of it — the symbolic story is still the reader's first experience; the Christian story is where it finds its deepest fulfillment. Where this review surfaces something that deepens, clarifies, or completes an edition's symbolic movement, it is recorded in that edition's own editorial record, the same way Dragon's editorial decisions are recorded in `Dragon_Editorial_Responses.md`.

**This lens now also produces a required page, not only a review.** The questions above govern the content of The Larger Story (Section 2), the page placed immediately after Recognition and before Reflection in every edition, built from three voices — Scripture, The Christian Tradition, Returning to the Story. Pages elsewhere in the edition (Recognition, Message Delivered, Living the Message) may still carry light echoes of this fulfillment, as Dragon's did before this page existed — see `Dragon_Christian_Fulfillment_Review.md` for the worked example. Whether those echoes name Scripture outright or let it shape the language unquoted is a literary choice, not a fixed rule — see Editorial Philosophy, "Scripture may be cited or may remain implicit." The Larger Story is where explicit citation is required; elsewhere it is available, not mandatory.

**The purpose of The Larger Story is theological, not a Scripture search.** Corrected 2026-07-06, after Dragon's own page went through six revisions because the underlying question kept changing underneath the wording. The symbolic figure already accomplishes its own purpose — it awakens recognition. The Larger Story exists to answer the question the figure raises, not to explain the figure, not to prove the figure with Scripture, and not to find a biblical story that resembles the figure. **The symbolic figure is not the Gospel. It prepares the heart to receive the Gospel.** That distinction governs every editorial decision on this page.

The corrected method, in strict order:

1. **Stop searching for passages that resemble the symbolic story, and stop searching for stories that parallel it.** Both approaches were tried on Dragon and set aside — narrative resemblance is not the test (see the further correction below).
2. **Identify the central Christian truth — the aspect of the Gospel — the symbolic recognition prepares the reader to receive.** This is an editorial and theological exercise, done before opening a concordance, and it does not yet name a passage.
3. **Only then determine whether Scripture, the Christian tradition, and the reader's response naturally grow from that truth.** If a passage naturally fulfills it, use it. **If no passage naturally fulfills that movement, keep searching. Do not force a text because it shares vocabulary or narrative shape.**

The governing question this method actually asks, stated the way it should be asked from now on: not *"What Scripture fits the symbolic story?"* but **"What aspect of the Gospel does the symbolic story prepare the reader to receive?"** Those are different questions, and the difference is the point.

**The full editorial movement this answers to, confirmed 2026-07-06 as governing the entire series, not only Dragon:** the symbolic story awakens recognition; Scripture reveals God's truth; the Christian tradition helps illuminate that truth; Christ remains the center; the reader is invited to live in that reality. And, stated as plainly as the method itself: the figure is never the source of wisdom, never a parallel gospel, never a biblical character — it is a symbolic witness that prepares the reader to hear the Christian story with fresh eyes. This page is never Christian content attached to an otherwise complete edition; no Figure Edition is "complete" without this movement in the first place.

**A further correction to the research-first method below: narrative shape is not the test either.** The paragraph immediately following this one, describing "research first, selection second," is retained because its caution against vocabulary-matching still holds — but its own instruction to evaluate a passage "by whether the narrative unfolds in the same progression as the symbolic story" is superseded by the method above. Dragon's own history shows why: Luke 8's Legion narrative unfolded in the same progression as Dragon's plot closely enough to survive a full dedicated research pass, and was still wrong, because narrative progression is not the same thing as theological center of gravity. The corrected test asks what Gospel truth the story points toward, not what story it resembles.

**The method is research first, selection second — never the reverse.** Identify the edition's central symbolic movement before opening a concordance. Research how voices from the Christian tradition — pastors, spiritual writers, theologians, classical sources — have described that same struggle, independent of Dragon or any other figure's specific vocabulary. Only then search for the biblical narrative that tells the same story — evaluated by whether the narrative unfolds in the same progression as the symbolic story, not by whether it shares similar words. Choosing a passage because it contains matching vocabulary ("peace," "reconciliation," "unity") is exactly the failure mode this method exists to prevent — see `Dragon_Christian_Tradition_Research.md` for the worked correction. The practical test, stated the way Susan phrased it for Dragon: if this symbolic edition were one of Jesus's own parables, where in the rest of Scripture would he take his disciples next? *(Superseded in part by the correction above — kept here, unedited, as the visible record of the method's own evolution.)*
