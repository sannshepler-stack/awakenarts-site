## Section 1 — Editorial

### Grammar

The prose throughout is largely clean. No grammatical errors in the conventional sense, and the poem's intentional fragmentation and non-standard syntax are features of the concrete poetry form, not errors. Three items deserve attention:

**1. Pronoun antecedent ambiguity, page 7.**
In the section "Rage as a Revealer, Not an Enemy," rage is first discussed as "it" ("it rises from wounds... it becomes a different kind of fuel"). The section closes: "He needs to be recognized." The shift from *it* to *He* is abrupt. It could refer to the dragon, to the person carrying rage, or to the rage itself. In context the dragon is likely intended, but the reader has just been reading about rage as the subject for three sentences. Suggest: "The dragon needs to be recognized" or "He — the dragon — needs to be recognized."

**2. Sentence construction, page 5.**
"The dragon may be thought of as dangerous, and in some cultures the opposite is true." The "opposite" refers to being thought of as dangerous, but the phrasing leaves room for ambiguity — opposite of dangerous, or opposite of being thought so? Suggest: "The dragon is often regarded as dangerous; in many cultures, the reverse is true." Cleaner and more decisive.

**3. Passive construction, page 10.**
"The dragon fulfills his duty by bringing the message that the parts were made to live as one." The nested structure ("bringing the message that") is slightly unwieldy. Consider: "The dragon fulfills his duty by declaring it: the parts were made to live as one." Or, since the page is ceremonial, simply allow it to stand — but flag for review.

---

### Punctuation

**1. Copyright placement, page 4.**
"SUSAN ANN SHEPLER©" is embedded within the shaped poem as the final line of its visual form. The copyright symbol is part of the poem's typography. This creates an ambiguity: is the © a poetic element or a legal notice? In a publication going to press, the copyright should appear either as a separate, distinct element below the poem (not within the poem shape itself) or on the Colophon page. Its current placement risks reading as part of the poem text to a first-time reader. Recommend: retain the author attribution within the poem shape; move the © notice to the Colophon as a standalone notice.

**2. Section 03 heading, page 7.**
The subsection "03 RETURN TO THE IMAGE" is titled "The Word That Reconciles" — which is also the title of the full page (Recognition II). This creates punctuation of a structural kind: the eye reads the same phrase twice, in two different contexts, on the same page, without distinguishing why. Addressed further under Structural.

---

### Wording

**Strongest passages in the edition:**
- Page 2: "Be still. Let the figure arrive before the explanation does." — precisely right
- Page 6 (Observe): "This is not a story about defeating a monster." — strong declarative
- Page 7 (Teaching Cautions, page 11 equivalent): "The hero does not destroy the dragon — he befriends the fire." — memorable
- Page 8 (instruction): "What matters here is not the quality of the writing but the quality of the attention." — the best single sentence in the edition
- Page 11: "Do not flatten the message into simple compromise: 'and' is not the same as splitting the difference." — precise and necessary

**Passages that could be sharpened:**

*Page 6, Observe section:* "A single dragon rests against a field of weathered cream — teal-blue, one wing raised, tail coiled beneath him." The color descriptor (teal-blue) arrives after a dash, as if in parentheses, then is immediately dropped as the sentence continues. It doesn't integrate smoothly. Suggest separating the color note: "A single dragon — teal-blue, painted on weathered cream — rests with one wing raised, tail coiled beneath him."

*Page 7, section 03:* "The word the dragon breathes is 'and.'" — Excellent line. The sentence that follows ("And. The hero does not destroy the dragon...") uses "And" as a standalone sentence-opening word for rhetorical effect, which works in speech but in print reads slightly incomplete. Not a change recommendation — flagging as a deliberate stylistic choice to confirm.

*Page 12, Colophon:* "This edition is one work within the AwakenArts Collection." The phrase "AwakenArts Collection" should be checked against current site terminology. The site refers to the site structure as a "Collection" page but may not formally use "the AwakenArts Collection" as a proper noun for the series. Confirm and standardize before future editions print.

---

### Consistency of Voice

The edition moves through four distinct registers in sequence:

1. **Poetic-immersive** (pages 1–4): atmospheric, imagistic, non-explanatory
2. **Informational** (page 5): plain, contextual
3. **Analytical-literary** (pages 6–7): third person, measured, intellectual
4. **Intimate-reflective** (pages 8–9): second person, direct, warm
5. **Declarative-ceremonial** (page 10): brief, bold, conclusive
6. **Practical** (page 11): instructional, third-person professional

This progression is intentional and works. The only friction point is the shift from the intimate second-person of page 8 ("Where Am I Fighting Myself?") back to third-person in page 10 ("The dragon fulfills his duty..."). Page 10 is clearly designed as a summation rather than a continued address to the reader, so the shift is appropriate — but the effect is a slight step back from the reader at the moment of closure. This is an editorial consideration, not a flaw.

---

### Repetition

**Intentional and effective:**
- "The parts were made to live as one" is used on page 4 (poem), page 10 (prose), and echoed in page 10's closing lines. This is earned repetition — it functions as the edition's thematic refrain.
- "Not this or that, but and" appears on the cover and is reprised on page 10. Proper bookending.
- The "&" symbol recurs across pages 7, 8, 9 (footer), 10. Effective motif deployment.

**Unintentional and worth addressing:**
- "The Word That Reconciles" appears as both the page title (Recognition II) and the heading for subsection 03 on the same page. This doubles the phrase without adding meaning. The subsection heading should be renamed. Suggested alternatives: "Recognizing the Whole," "The Resolution," "Two Things, One Reality."

---

### Clarity

**Page 6, Observe section:** The Observe instruction directs the reader to examine the figure — but the figure image (page 3) is three pages back. In digital reading, this requires a conscious page-jump. In print, a reader must physically flip backward. Consider whether the Observe prompt should reference "the figure you encountered on page 3" explicitly, or whether a small thumbnail of the dragon figure should appear within the Recognition section as a visual anchor for group/classroom contexts.

**Page 8 vs. page 9, journaling instruction conflict:** Page 8 opens: "These prompts are for your private journal." Page 9 then offers writing lines "in this book, or in your own journal." These two instructions together are not contradictory but they establish slightly different expectations in sequence. Recommend revising page 8's instruction to: "These prompts are for your own reflection — in this book or your own journal." This removes the word "private" (which subtly discourages writing in the book) and aligns with page 9's invitation.

**Page 11, external document reference:** "See the separate Dragon Workshop document for the full nine-stage timing table and stage-by-stage facilitator script." This cross-reference should be verified: does this document currently exist in distributable form? If not, the reference creates a dead end for every facilitator who holds this edition. Either the document should be completed and linked, or the reference should be modified to: "A full facilitation guide, including a nine-stage timing table and session script, is available separately at AwakenArts.com."

## Section 2 — Structural

### Page Order

The 12-page sequence is:

**Cover → Threshold → Figure → Poem → Motif → Recognition I → Recognition II → Reflection → Journaling → Message → Facilitator → Colophon**

This order is sound. The fundamental arc — encounter, understand, internalize, receive — is well-structured. One structural question worth raising for the production standard:

**The Motif page (5) follows the Poem (4).** The reader encounters the shaped, emotionally intense poem and then immediately receives informational cultural context about the dragon symbol. This sequence produces a useful de-escalation before the deeper Recognition analysis, but it also interrupts the poem's emotional momentum. An alternative sequence — placing the Motif page before the poem (Cover → Threshold → Figure → Motif → Poem → Recognition) — would give readers cultural context before the emotional encounter, potentially deepening their reading of the poem. This is a legitimate editorial consideration for future editions, not a correction for Dragon.

---

### Transitions

**Strong transitions:**
- Page 7 (Recognition II, analytical) → Page 8 (Where It Meets You, dark navy, intimate): The shift from cream analytical to dark navy reflective is the single most effective transition in the edition. The inward turn is visually and tonally enacted simultaneously.
- Page 10 (Message Delivered, dark navy) → Page 11 (Facilitator Notes, cream): The return to cream and the shift to professional register is clean. The "NOT FOR PARTICIPANT DISTRIBUTION" banner signals the change of audience unmistakably.

**Transitions that need attention:**

*Page 10 → Page 11:* The Message Delivered page closes the participant experience at the highest possible note — the edition's thesis, the large gold "&," the ceremonial final lines. Page 11 (Facilitator Notes) immediately follows. For participants reading the print edition, the turn of a page from "Not this or that. But and." to "NOT FOR PARTICIPANT DISTRIBUTION" creates a jarring tonal drop. A blank page (or even a half-page of white space) between pages 10 and 11 would protect the landing. In PDF distribution, this matters less if participant and facilitator versions are separate files — but if distributed together, the transition needs a buffer.

*Page 2 (Encounter Threshold) → Page 3 (The Figure):* The transition from full-bleed fire/volcanic atmosphere to the navy page with the framed watercolor is deliberate — darkness to contained presence. It works, but the shift in background texture (photographic vs. flat navy) is abrupt. This is by design; noting it here so future editions consider whether the same approach serves the tonal need for each specific figure.

---

### Flow

The section labeling system creates unintended confusion. The word "ENCOUNTER" is used in the section header for five different pages: page 2 (the threshold introduction), pages 3 and 4 (the figure and poem, via "ENCOUNTER · THE FIGURE" and "ENCOUNTER · THE POEM"), and pages 8, 9, and 10 (the reflection, journaling, and message).

Pages 8, 9, and 10 are not encounters with the figure in the sense that pages 2, 3, and 4 are. By page 8, the reader has already encountered, analyzed, and recognized the figure. Pages 8–10 are the inward application and closing of the encounter — not its opening.

Reusing "ENCOUNTER" as the section label for pages 8–10 makes the edition's internal navigation ambiguous. A reader who looks at the section label to locate where they are in the progression will find "ENCOUNTER" appears at the beginning and again near the end, with "RECOGNITION" in between — creating the impression that the encounter restarts after recognition, rather than deepens through it.

**Recommendation for production standard:** Rename the section labels for pages 8–10. Candidate label: "REFLECTION" (pages 8–9) and "ENCOUNTER · THE MESSAGE" → "RESPONSE · THE MESSAGE" (page 10). Or collapse pages 8–10 under a new label: "APPLICATION" or "THE INVITATION." This decision should be made once and held consistently across all future editions.

---

### Missing Components

**1. No table of contents or section map.** For a 12-page edition that moves through five or six distinct sections, a brief contents listing on page 2 or as part of the cover verso would help facilitators navigate when using the edition in group settings. Even a one-line listing in small text ("The Figure · The Poem · A Motif · Recognition · Reflection · Guided Journaling · Message Delivered · Facilitator Notes") would serve this purpose without adding a full page.

**2. No author biography.** The Colophon names the website and copyright holder but offers no sentence about Susan Ann Shepler as the author and artist. For an edition in which the author's voice and personal symbolic language are central, one sentence of biographical grounding is appropriate. Even: "Susan Ann Shepler is a certified Transformative Language Artist and the creator of the AwakenArts symbolic edition series."

**3. No date of publication.** The Colophon carries no year. For an edition positioned as a permanent, print-quality publication, the year of first publication should appear either on the Colophon page or as part of the copyright line.

**4. The external workshop document cross-reference (addressed under Clarity).** This is also a structural gap: the Facilitator Notes reference a resource that may not yet exist as a separate distributable document. This creates an incomplete facilitation package.

---

### Unnecessary Components

No components appear unnecessary. Every page carries its weight. The "&" ornament on pages 7 and 8 (centered, mid-size) is borderline — it functions as both separator and thematic motif, but in those locations reads more like a placeholder than a deliberate statement. Once the motif's use is consistent and explained by page 10, pages 7 and 8 feel retroactively meaningful. No change recommended — but the "&"'s early appearances should be considered intentional rather than incidental in the production standard.

## Section 3 — Visual

### Typography

The edition uses approximately seven distinct typographic treatments across its content pages. This is a rich system, and it is internally consistent — the same treatment is used for the same element type every time it appears.

The treatments, in order of visual weight:
1. Large bold serif — page/section title (navy on cream; white on navy)
2. Gold small caps — section label (e.g., "RECOGNITION · I"), subsection numbered labels (e.g., "01EXPLORE THE PATTERN")
3. Bold serif — subsection headings within pages
4. Regular serif italic — pull quotes (terracotta from poem; cream/white on dark pages; gold for thematic closings)
5. Regular serif — body text (navy on cream; white or cream on navy)
6. Small italic serif — instructional framing ("These prompts are for your journal...")
7. Small caps footnote — running footer

**Concern: The numbered subsection labels.** The format "01EXPLORE THE PATTERN" fuses the number and the label without a typographic separator. In the current layout, the "01" superscripts above the "E" of "EXPLORE," creating a visual overlap. This is legible at screen resolution but may tighten at print resolution, particularly if font hinting changes at smaller sizes. Recommend: add a space or an em-dash between the number and the label — "01 · EXPLORE THE PATTERN" — or typeset the number as a separate element above the label on its own baseline.

**Note on page 5 title:** "DRAGON: A MOTIF" uses a colon in a display heading. This reads more as a category label than a page title and gives the page a slightly administrative quality compared to the more literary titles elsewhere ("The Illusion of Opposition," "The Word That Reconciles"). Consider "The Dragon as Motif" or simply "A Motif" to match the page's quiet, informational register.

---

### Spacing

**Page 6 (Recognition I):** The body of section 01 ends approximately 35–40% from the bottom of the page. A large field of blank cream space follows. This white space is substantial and may read as an incomplete layout rather than intentional breathing room, particularly if the reader does not know the section continues on the next page. If content cannot expand to fill the space, a subtle design element (a thin horizontal rule, a repeated "&" in a very light weight, or a small icon) at the mid-point between the last paragraph and the page bottom would give the white space intentionality.

**Page 7 (Recognition II):** The "&" separator at the bottom of the page floats in a large field of white below the last body paragraph. Same concern as page 6 — the lower half of the page is largely empty. This is the most visually unresolved spread in the edition. The "&" at the page bottom, while thematically correct, does not visually anchor the space.

**Page 4 (The Poem):** Between the shaped poem text and the pull quote below, there is a generous band of white space. This separation feels appropriate — it gives the eye time to recover from the visual complexity of the shaped poem before receiving the summary quote. No change recommended here; flagging as intentional.

**Page 9 (Guided Journaling):** The writing lines are consistent and appropriately spaced. The bottom quarter of the page is blank (after the last line). This is standard for a journal page — the lines don't need to fill the full bleed. No concern.

---

### Margins

Content pages (cream background) maintain generous, consistent margins. The live text area is well-centered and proportioned to the page dimensions.

The framed figure image (page 3) has slightly more margin below the frame than above it — the frame sits slightly high on the page, with "THE DRAGON" title below it given ample breathing room. This top-weighted image placement creates the impression that the figure is looking out from an elevated position, which suits the dragon's character. Intentional or incidental, it works.

The framed motif image (page 5) has similar top-weighting but is followed by only two paragraphs of text, which leaves the page's lower third open. See spacing notes above.

---

### Hierarchy

The hierarchy is clear and successfully maintained across all content pages:

Section label (gold small caps) → Title (large bold serif) → Gold rule → [Pull quote in terracotta/italic] → Content body (numbered subsections with bold headings)

One inconsistency: page 8 (Where It Meets You) opens with the section label "ENCOUNTER" and the title "Where It Meets You" — consistent with other pages. But it does not use the numbered subsection system (01, 02, 03) for the three journaling prompts; instead, it uses a gold heading and a short em-dash rule above each prompt. This is visually appropriate for the intimate register of the page, but it does mean that the three prompts are not numbered, making it slightly harder for a group facilitator to direct participants to "prompt two" or "prompt three" in a discussion. Adding numbers to the prompts (or letters: A, B, C) would improve group use without changing the visual tone.

---

### Image Placement

**Page 3 (The Figure, watercolor):** Well-composed. The cream mat and thin border frame the watercolor painting with dignity. The figure is centered and fully visible. The navy background gives the framed image a gallery quality. This is the standard for figure presentation in the edition.

**Page 5 (The Motif):** Uses the same framed-image treatment as page 3, on a cream background. The motif image (black, stippled/dotted dragon silhouette) is visually strong and distinct from the watercolor. The consistency of framing between figure and motif creates a useful visual relationship between the two images even though they are aesthetically different.

**Page 6 (Recognition I, partial photo):** The atmospheric landscape runs full-bleed across the top third of the page. The transition to the cream content area below is a clean hard edge. This editorial-magazine approach is effective and visually dramatic.

**Page 2 (Encounter Introduction, full-bleed photo):** The volcanic/fire landscape with orange sky and embers is the strongest atmospheric image in the edition. Its drama prepares the reader for the intensity of the poem and sets the tone for the Dragon's symbolic character. Standard to consider: the atmospheric threshold image should be chosen to embody the figure's emotional and symbolic character before the figure is revealed. Dragon's image does this exactly.

---

### Visual Balance

The edition's dark-light rhythm across 12 pages:
- Dark: 1 (photo), 2 (dark navy), 3 (navy), 8 (navy), 10 (navy) — 5 pages
- Cream: 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 11, 12 — 7 pages

The edition opens in darkness (pages 1–3) and gradually moves toward cream (pages 4–9), then returns to darkness at the closing declaration (page 10), before completing in cream (pages 11–12). This arc mirrors the content: the encounter begins in the unknown, the analysis occurs in the light, the reflection returns to darkness, and the conclusion settles in clarity.

This dark-light rhythm is one of the edition's most successfully designed features and should become an explicit production standard. Future editions should map their dark and cream pages against the same emotional arc: opening in darkness or atmosphere, moving through analysis in light, closing the participant experience in darkness, completing in cream.

## Section 4 — Educational

### Learning Pages (Recognition I and II)

The two Recognition pages form the analytical core of the edition. Their three-step structure — 01 Explore the Pattern → 02 Develop the Insight → 03 Return to the Image — is the edition's most clearly educational element, and it is well-designed.

The progression is pedagogically sound:
- Step 01 grounds the reader in what the poem and image show (observation)
- Step 02 develops the meaning (interpretation)
- Step 03 returns to the image with new eyes (re-integration)

This observe-interpret-return structure is not labeled as a methodology — it's embedded in the content naturally. That is the right decision for a publication whose reading experience should not feel like a curriculum.

**Concern:** The Observe prompt (page 6) asks the reader to look at the figure, but the figure is three pages behind them. In physical print, this can be managed by keeping page 3 open alongside page 6 — but this requires two-handed use of the book. In digital PDF reading, it requires a deliberate page-jump. Consider adding a small reproduction of the dragon figure (perhaps 1.5–2 inch width) as a thumbnail within the Observe section on page 6, positioned adjacent to the Observe text, with a caption noting its relationship to page 3. This serves group facilitation contexts especially, where a projected or printed page 6 may be on screen while participants cannot access page 3 simultaneously.

**Recognition I vs. II asymmetry:** Recognition I (page 6) carries an atmospheric photo in its top third, a terracotta pull quote from the poem, a labeled Observe section in italic, and one numbered subsection. Recognition II (page 7) carries only text — no photo, no pull quote, two numbered subsections. This asymmetry is not a flaw in itself, but it means Recognition I reads as more visually resolved than Recognition II. If the production standard calls for Recognition II to be text-only, this should be recorded explicitly. If not, Recognition II could benefit from a small visual element (a rule, an ornamental mark, or a second line of poem-derived text in terracotta) to match page 6's visual completeness.

---

### Reflection Pages (Where It Meets You)

Page 8's three prompts are the edition's most direct invitation to personal application, and they are the best-written practical content in the edition. The prompts move from broad to specific to integrative:

1. "Where Am I Fighting Myself?" — establishes the personal relevance of the figure's conflict
2. "What Part of Me Have I Called the Enemy?" — deepens the recognition
3. "What Would 'And' Look Like Here?" — asks for constructive response

This progression mirrors the Recognition pages' three-step arc at the personal level. The prompts are appropriately phrased in first person (the heading) with second-person support text below. The gold separator rule between each prompt gives visual breathing room without adding page length.

**One wording concern (from the Editorial section, repeated here for educational emphasis):** Page 8's instruction says "These prompts are for your private journal," which subtly discourages writing in the edition itself. Page 9 then opens up: "Write below — in this book, or in your own journal." These two instructions should be harmonized so that both pages give the reader the same permission from the start.

---

### Guided Journaling (Page 9)

The journaling page is spare and well-designed. The instruction "Return to whichever question stayed with you the most. Stay with it longer. Let it open further" is the right level of guidance — enough to direct the reader without prescribing the response.

**Opportunity:** The journaling page is the only page in the participant section that carries no "&" ornament within its live content area (the running footer carries the "&" as part of the footer system, but not as a positioned ornamental element within the page). The "&" on pages 7, 8, and 10 creates a chain that skips page 9. Whether intentional or incidental, placing a subtle "&" at the bottom of the journaling page (above the footer) would complete the chain and signal that this page is still within the same thematic space. A very light-weight or slightly smaller "&" would work without visually competing with the lined writing area.

---

### Facilitator Notes (Page 11)

The Facilitator Notes page is well-conceived and contains several notable strengths:

**Strongest elements:**
- **Discussion Objectives** are clear and double-pointed: recognize personal conflict, close with attention on the image of reconciliation (not a verdict about who was right). This is precise and serves the facilitator directly.
- **Teaching Cautions** are the best-written practical guidance in the edition. The three cautions — don't portray the dragon as evil, don't collapse into defeat-the-dragon, don't flatten to simple compromise — address the three most likely group misreadings. This level of precision should be standard in every edition's Facilitator Notes.
- **Facilitator Note callout box** is appropriately brief and addresses a real facilitation risk (participant disclosure). Its language is respectful and clear.

**Areas to strengthen:**

*Callout box placement:* The Facilitator Note callout currently appears at the top-right of the two-column layout, meaning it is read after the Discussion Objectives in left-to-right column order. Because the callout addresses safety — the most critical facilitation concern — it should appear first, before Discussion Objectives and Group Questions. Facilitators need to hold this awareness before they engage the content questions, not after. Recommend: place the Facilitator Note callout at the top of the page, spanning both columns, before the two-column content begins.

*"NOT FOR PARTICIPANT DISTRIBUTION" notice:* Currently a full-width gold banner immediately below the page title, this notice visually interrupts the flow from title to content. Recommend: move the notice to the page header (above the title, in the same position as other pages' section labels) or to the footer. The title "Facilitator Notes" is itself sufficient to signal the page's purpose; the distribution notice doesn't need to be the second element a facilitator reads.

*Numbered Group Questions do not match Reflection Prompts:* The edition contains three Reflection prompts (page 8) and four Group Questions (page 11). This asymmetry is appropriate — the group discussion may go further than the individual reflection — but it is worth documenting in the production standard, since future editions may need to coordinate these two sets of questions to ensure the group conversation can build on the individual reflection without simply repeating it.

*Formal Learning Objectives are absent.* The Discussion Objectives state what the facilitator should accomplish, but there are no stated learning outcomes for participants ("After this encounter, participants will be able to..."). Facilitators working in structured educational contexts — church adult education, retreat programs, workshops — will frequently need a formal outcomes statement to justify or contextualize the use of this material. Consider adding one sentence of learning outcomes to the Discussion Objectives: "Participants will be invited to recognize a pattern of internal opposition in their own lives and consider the possibility of integration rather than resolution."

---

### Message Delivered (Page 10)

Page 10 is the edition's most successful page. The design, content, and typography work together at every level:

- The dark navy field creates a return to the atmosphere of the edition's opening
- The large gold "&" is both the thematic symbol and a word — it does not merely illustrate the message, it is the message
- "Not this or that." (italic, gold) followed by "But and." (bold, white) delivers the edition's thesis at its most compressed

**One concern about the section label:** Page 10 is labeled "ENCOUNTER · THE MESSAGE." As discussed under Structural, this reuse of "ENCOUNTER" for the closing declaration is inconsistent with the section labeling of pages 6 and 7 (which use "RECOGNITION"). By the time the reader reaches page 10, they have completed an Encounter, two Recognitions, a Reflection, and a Journaling session. Page 10 is not another encounter — it is a summation or response. Recommend: rename to "RESPONSE · THE MESSAGE" or "RECOGNITION · THE MESSAGE" or simply "THE MESSAGE."

## Section 5 — Production Standards

*Derived from Dragon Figure Edition No. 01. Dragon establishes the foundation of the Figure Edition series; future editions should confirm, refine, and strengthen these standards rather than simply repeat them unchanged.*

*Each standard is tagged with one of three types:*
- ***Essential principle*** *— defines the Figure Edition; does not change without strong reason*
- ***Working standard*** *— current production approach; may mature through production experience*
- ***Production preference*** *— open to refinement; not a requirement*

*Governing principle: The objective is consistency of experience — not mechanical uniformity. Every Figure Edition shares a common structure, educational method, and visual language while allowing each figure, poem, and symbolic encounter to determine its own artistic expression.*

---

### Standard 1 — Sequence and Structure
The sequence defines the edition's structure. Page count follows from the sequence — not the other way around. Dragon required 12 pages; a figure with greater depth may require 14. The sequence below is the standard; the count is the result.

Every Figure Edition shall follow this sequence in order:

1. Cover
2. Encounter Threshold (atmospheric full-bleed introduction)
3. The Figure (figure artwork, framed, on dark background)
4. The Poem (complete poem, shaped or typeset as appropriate to the work)
5. The Motif (companion symbolic image that expands or illuminates the Figure's symbolic world)
6. Recognition I (analysis, part one — "Explore the Pattern" and "Develop the Insight")
7. Recognition II (analysis, part two — "Return to the Image" and thematic resolution)
8. Where It Meets You (reflection prompts — minimum three)
9. Guided Journaling (writing lines with instruction)
10. Message Delivered (closing declaration, symbolic visual)
11. Facilitator Notes (marked NOT FOR PARTICIPANT DISTRIBUTION)
12. Colophon

Additional pages, if a figure requires them, are inserted within the sequence at the point of greatest need — not appended. A blank separator page may be added between the last participant page and Facilitator Notes if both are distributed as a single file.

**Amendment, Dragon Figure Edition 11:** For any figure with a separate Facilitator/Companion resource, item 11 (Facilitator Notes) is removed from this sequence and a new page — a continuing-cultivation closing page per Standard 22 — is inserted between Message Delivered and the Colophon. See the Dragon Figure Edition 11 Revision Record below for Dragon's specific sequence and page titles.

### Standard 2 — Color Palette
*Working standard — current production standard for the series.*

All Figure Editions shall use the following four-color palette:

- **Deep navy** (#1C2B3A or equivalent): background for immersive pages, primary heading text on cream pages
- **Terracotta/amber** (warm orange-red): poem-derived pull quotes only — quotations drawn directly from the poem
- **Gold** (#8B6914 or equivalent): section labels, horizontal rules, subsection numbers, thematic ornaments, website URL
- **Cream** (#FAF7F2 or equivalent): page background for content pages, reversed text on dark backgrounds

No additional primary colors shall be introduced in content pages. Individual atmospheric images may introduce additional natural colors where the character of the figure requires it. Tints of these four colors are permitted for subtle differentiation (callout box backgrounds, etc.).

### Standard 9 — Recognition Pages

3. **Develop the Insight** — deepening of the recognition
4. **Return to the Image** — integration of understanding back to the figure

### Standard 10 — Reflection and Journaling

### Standard 19 — The Facilitator's Role

Facilitator Notes shall be written with this principle as their governing purpose. Teaching Cautions, Discussion Objectives, and Group Questions should all serve this aim: holding the space open, not directing participants toward predetermined conclusions.

### Standard 21 — Reader-Facing Language
*Essential principle.*

Every element a reader actually encounters — page titles, section labels, running headers, instructional copy — shall be written in language addressed to the reader, not in the vocabulary used to build the edition. Production and development terms ("Encounter Threshold," "the Figure," "the Motif," "Colophon," "Symbolic Edition") are useful during construction but do not belong on the page a participant holds. The test: a page title should answer the reader's implicit question, "What am I doing here?" — not describe the page's function within the production system.

This does not require abandoning evocative language (words like "Recognition" and "Reflection" pass the test — they name an experience, not a production category). It requires checking every reader-visible label against the test before an edition is finalized.

*Added 2026-07-01 per Susan's direction, Dragon Figure Edition 11 revision.*

### Standard 22 — Continuing Cultivation
*Essential principle.*

Every Figure Edition shall leave the reader with something they can recognize, reflect upon, and use in the ordinary movements of life. The value of an edition is not only the recognition it produces during the reading, but the growth it invites afterward — in the days and weeks that follow, not only in the sitting.

This shall take the form of a closing page, following Message Delivered and preceding the Colophon, that offers the reader a small number of forward-carrying movements — noticing, returning, practicing, continuing — rather than further interpretation or additional teaching. This page shall remain participant-facing: private and communal use both, but never dependent on a facilitator. A closing set of three to five short, open questions — answerable alone as journal prompts or aloud as conversation starters — may be included on this page as the edition's only accommodation to small-group use; it shall not expand into facilitation guidance, timing, or leader instruction, which remain reserved for the edition's separate Facilitator/Companion resource where one exists.

Dragon's implementation of this standard is the "Living the Message" page — see the Dragon Figure Edition 11 Revision Record below.

*Added 2026-07-01 per Susan's direction, Dragon Figure Edition 11 revision.*

**Amendment, same date:** see Standard 23 (Recognition, Not Interpretation) for the deeper principle this standard implements, and the revised "Living the Message" text below reoriented around it.

### Standard 23 — Recognition, Not Interpretation (The Parable Principle)
*Essential principle. Governs the editorial philosophy of every Figure Edition.*

AwakenArts Figure Editions are not designed to teach symbolic interpretation. They are designed to cultivate recognition. Like a parable, the value of the encounter is not exhausted when the book is closed — it is discovered afterward, as the reader begins recognizing the same pattern within ordinary life.

The edition's closing movement (Standard 22) shall be governed by this principle: it should ask whether the symbolic encounter has created a new way of seeing that the reader can now recognize in ordinary life, not whether the reader can apply, practice, or complete anything. Language throughout this movement shall be invitational rather than instructional — questions, not exercises; noticing, not assignments.

Every edition's closing pages are the bridge between the symbolic world of the edition and the reader's lived experience. The question an edition leaves the reader with should not be "What does this symbol mean?" but "Where have you begun to see this pattern in your own life?" This is a shift from symbolic interpretation to a continuing experience, and it governs the editorial philosophy of every Figure Edition going forward, not only Dragon's.

*Added 2026-07-01 per Susan's direction — "Reorient the Closing Movement Around Recognition in Ordinary Life."*
