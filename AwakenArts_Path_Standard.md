# AwakenArts — The Path Standard

**Status: Governing correction. Issued 2026-08-20, per Susan Ann Shepler's direct instruction. Governs the whole of AwakenArts — every page, Figure, workshop, and piece of writing — not a single Figure, edition, or page.**

---

## The Correction

"The Path" already appears in the governing set — `AwakenArts_Production_Charter.md`, Addendum A, names "The Path's role" among the ecosystem's named elements, and the live site nav carries a Path item (redirected in `next.config.js` to `/studio`). Neither location states, in one sentence, what the Path actually *is* structurally, or gives a portable test for whether a given piece of work belongs to it. Work has been commissioned, built, and evaluated Figure-by-Figure and page-by-page, without a shared standard for what makes an element real AwakenArts work rather than decoration, scope creep, or a page that exists because it is interesting rather than because it is needed.

This document supplies both: a definition, and a test.

---

## The Standard

**The AwakenArts Path is the sequence of stones by which a person can move from encountering symbolic material toward greater capacity to understand the images, language, patterns, and personal myths through which human experience takes shape.**

Read this precisely, because each clause carries weight:

- **A sequence of stones**, not a funnel. A stone is a discrete place along the movement — not necessarily a page, not necessarily a Figure, not necessarily a single session. Some stones may be small (a single encounter); some may be substantial (a whole Figure's workshop). What makes something a stone is not its size but its place in the sequence: a person is somewhere before it, and somewhere else — able to do something they could not do before — after it.
- **From encountering symbolic material toward greater capacity.** The destination is not "having consumed more AwakenArts content." It is a *capacity* — the person's own growing ability to read image, language, pattern, and their own personal myth — that deepens stone by stone. A person who has read three Figure Editions but developed no greater capacity to work with symbolic material on their own has not moved along the Path; they have accumulated encounters.
- **The images, language, patterns and personal myths through which human experience takes shape.** This names what the capacity is *for* — not aesthetic appreciation of AwakenArts' own work specifically, but a durable capacity the person carries into their own life, their own images, their own story. The Figure is the material the capacity is built on; the capacity itself is what the Path exists to develop.

---

## The Test

**Every AwakenArts element — a page, a Figure, a workshop stage, a piece of copy, a single image, a line of facilitator script — must do at least one of three things:**

1. **Help set a stone.** Contribute to building or defining a place along the Path — research, authored symbolic content, a new Figure's development, a structural addition to the sequence itself.
2. **Help someone cross a stone.** Actively move a participant's own capacity forward at that point — facilitation, a Reader sequence, a workshop exercise, a live encounter.
3. **Give them a reason to continue.** Sustain forward motion without itself being a stone — threshold copy, previews, invitations, wayfinding. (This is the same work already named in `AGENTS.md`'s description of the website as "an editorial threshold... every design decision, piece of copy, and navigation element serves the work.")

**An element that does none of the three does not belong, regardless of how polished, interesting, or well-executed it is on its own terms.** This is now the standing test for scope — not only when commissioning new work, but at review. If a slide, a page, or a stage cannot be named as setting a stone, helping someone cross one, or giving a reason to continue, the right response is to cut it, not to find a justification for keeping it.

## Standing Relationship to Other Governing Documents

- **`AwakenArts_Production_Charter.md`** remains supreme. This document sharpens and makes testable a concept the Charter's Addendum A already names ("The Path's role") — it does not compete with or amend the Charter's authority.
- **`AwakenArts_Editorial_Philosophy.md`**'s reader journey (Encounter → Notice → Recognize → Reflect → Respond → Return to Ordinary Life) is very likely the shape of a single stone, or of the sequence within a Figure — worth confirming explicitly in a future pass, not decided here.
- **`AwakenArts_Figure_Research_and_Development_Standard.md`** governs how the *content* of a stone is researched and authored before it is built. This document governs whether the resulting stone — and everything built around it — actually belongs on the Path at all. The two are complementary: one is about getting a stone's content right; this one is about whether something should be a stone, a crossing, an invitation, or nothing.
- **The New AwakenArts Paradigm's** ten-stage workshop arc (`/Users/sashe/Desktop/Dragon Pass One.docx`) is likely itself one stone, built from several smaller internal movements — also worth confirming in a future pass rather than assumed here.

## Applying This Now

This Standard governs going forward. It does not, by itself, require auditing or removing any existing page, Figure, or piece of copy — but any future new element should be tested against it before being built, the same way the Figure Research Standard requires research before script.

Two open questions are flagged here rather than resolved, because they touch site architecture and creative direction that are Susan's call, not mine to settle by writing this document:

- **Is "the Path" now the name for the whole AwakenArts architecture** — every Figure, every workshop, the entire sequence a person walks — **distinct from and larger than the current nav item and page** currently called "The Path" (redirected to `/studio`)? Or are these still meant to be the same thing, one page among several?
- **Should the site's navigation and information architecture eventually be reorganized to reflect the Path as its organizing structure**, rather than one item alongside Symbols, Collection, Gallery, Journal, and About? This is a real design question this Standard raises but does not answer.

Whether and when to pursue either question is Susan's decision.
