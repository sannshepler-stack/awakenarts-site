## The Central WHY

*"We use symbols, images, poetry, and writing to observe something we do every day: make meaning from what we encounter. We notice not only what we think, but what our minds are doing as we think it. When something becomes recognizable, we can put it into conscious language; once we can recognize and name it, we have more choice about how we operate from it."*

Every stage below is built to let the participant **live** this before anyone names it. Nothing in the blueprint states the metacognitive premise aloud until Meta Shift (stage 6) — Orientation gives the WHY, not the mechanism, exactly as the Paradigm document specifies: *"Do not give away what Dragon will reveal. Preserve mystery about the discovery — not about the purpose."*

## Governing Constraints Carried Through Every Stage

**Readiness before depth.** AwakenArts does not ask for depth before it has created readiness for depth. The workshop's first three stages (Orientation, Encounter, Association) are deliberately light — accessible, curious, low-stakes — before Story/Poem and Disruption begin asking anything of the participant. Nothing psychologically or symbolically heavy appears before minute fifteen.

**Object level / meta level.** Object level: *"What am I looking at?"* Meta level: *"What is my own mind doing as I encounter it?"* The workshop's entire spine is the deliberate, staged move from the first question to the second — never taught as a concept, always produced as an experience.

**Entertainment is not decoration — it is a design test.** Every stage below is built to pass one standing question: ***would I want to keep watching this if nobody required me to?*** The toolkit used throughout is the Paradigm document's own list: striking imagery, poetry, story, curiosity, anticipation, visual reveal, juxtaposition, surprise, ambiguity followed by recognition, an image returning later with altered significance, and questions that change what participants thought they were seeing. Each stage names which of these tools is doing the work.

**Format and timing.** Designed as a 90-minute session, matching the timing discipline already established in the old nine-stage Workshop Plan (which protected Reflection as the single longest stage so instruction never crowds out recognition). A 60-minute compression note is given at the end of each stage where the old plan's own compression pattern applies cleanly to the new ten-stage arc.

## Stage 1 — Orientation: Why Are We Here?

**Purpose.** Give the participant the WHY without giving away the discovery. This stage exists to set the room's contract — what kind of time this will be — not to teach anything about Dragon.

**What the participant sees/hears.** No dragon imagery yet. A quiet room, Susan speaking directly and briefly, no slides beyond perhaps a title card. Nothing symbolic is shown.

**What Susan says/does.** This is the single largest genuine content gap in the whole system — the old Workshop Plan allotted five minutes to Welcome and five to Orientation and wrote nothing for either. Recommended opening language, built from the Paradigm's WHY and the Guide's container-setting register (*"There's nothing to figure out right away, and nothing you notice is too small to mention"*):

> "We use symbols, images, poetry, and writing to observe something we do every day: make meaning from what we encounter. We notice not only what we think, but what our minds are doing as we think it. Today we'll spend time with one figure — a dragon. I'm not going to tell you what it means. That's not because I'm withholding something from you. It's because the point isn't the dragon's meaning — it's watching what your own mind does when it meets him. There's nothing to figure out right away, and nothing you notice is too small to mention. When something becomes recognizable, we can put it into language — and once we can name it, we have more choice about what we do with it. Let's begin."

**What the participant does.** Listens. No task yet — this is the only stage in the arc that asks nothing of the participant except attention.

**Existing material to use.** None exists as spoken content. The Paradigm document's WHY section supplies the raw language above almost verbatim.

**Material to move/reframe rather than rewrite.** Nothing — there is no prior Welcome/Orientation script to reframe; the old Workshop Plan confirms both stages were blank rows.

**Genuinely new material.** The entire spoken Orientation script. This is the clearest, cheapest, highest-leverage piece of missing content in the system — five minutes of language, not a redesign.

**Timing.** 5 minutes. Not compressible below 3–4 minutes even at 60 minutes; the WHY has to land before Encounter or the whole arc loses its frame.

**Transition to Encounter.** Susan stops talking. A beat of silence — 3–5 seconds, deliberately shorter than the silences later in the session, since nothing has been shown yet — then the image appears without further comment.

**Entertainment/engagement mechanism.** Curiosity and restraint. The tool here is withholding: naming that something is coming without naming what. This is the "preserve mystery about the discovery, not the purpose" instruction operating as craft, not just doctrine.

## Stage 2 — Encounter: Give the Mind an Object

**Purpose.** Object-level experience, first exposure. The participant looks before anything is explained.

**What the participant sees/hears.** The Dragon image — the existing framed watercolor artwork already produced for the Edition's "The Image" page (dark ground, cream mat) — shown in silence. No poem yet, no title, no framing language. This matches the Companion's own Encounter instruction exactly: *"Present the dragon — poem and image, where available — in silence, without introduction. Do not explain the symbolism beforehand."*

**What Susan says/does.** Nothing, at first — she shows the image and holds silence. The Guide is explicit that this needs to be real silence, not a token pause: *"give it real silence before any discussion — at minimum a full minute for an individual session, often two or three for a group... your own ease with the silence is doing as much work as the silence itself."* After the silence, one open observational question, per the Guide's laddering rung 1: *"What do you notice?"* or *"Where did your attention go first?"* — never *"What does this remind you of?"* at this stage, which nudges toward association too early.

**What the participant does.** Looks. In a group setting, per the Guide's sequencing guidance, each person offers one observation in turn — not yet interpretation — before the floor opens, so quieter participants get space before the room's most vocal reader shapes the conversation.

**Existing material to use.** The Edition's existing "Image" artwork — already produced, already vetted against the Visual Standard. **KEEP outright**, per Goal 1's own classification.

**Material to move/reframe.** The Companion's explicit facilitator note belongs here verbatim, moved rather than rewritten: *"do not name 'animus,' 'shadow,' or 'and' during this stage. Those belong to Learning, later in the sequence."* Under the new arc this note still holds, but "Learning" is now distributed across Meta Shift, Recognition, and Conscious Language rather than living in one Learning block — the caution travels, the destination changes.

**Genuinely new material.** None for content. What's new is pacing discipline: the old plan gave Encounter 10 minutes total across silence and initial reaction; this blueprint recommends holding at least 60–90 seconds of pure silence before the first question, longer than most facilitators find comfortable on a first pass.

**Timing.** 8 minutes (60–90 seconds silence, remainder for a first round of observations, no interpretation yet).

**Transition to Association.** Susan does not correct or extend any observation offered. She simply opens the frame wider: "Let's stay with that a little longer — what else does this bring to mind?"

**Entertainment/engagement mechanism.** Visual reveal and curiosity. The tool is the same one striking-imagery-first instinct that already exists in the Edition's "Begin" page ("Be still. Let the figure arrive before the explanation does.") — confirmed as already KEEP-worthy in Goal 1. Nothing new needs inventing here; it needs to be staged live rather than read on a page.

## Stage 3 — Association: Let the Mind Work

**Purpose.** Let interpretations form, unprompted and uncorrected. This is the arc's first genuinely missing beat — Goal 1 flagged Association as "largely missing as a distinct beat, currently folded into Recognition." Here it becomes its own stop.

**What the participant sees/hears.** The same image remains visible, now joined by Susan's open questions. Still no poem, no title, no theology.

**What Susan says/does.** Uses the Guide's rung-2 laddering questions: *"What does that bring to mind?"* and, if needed, the deliberately open cousin the Guide recommends over "Does this remind you of ___?": *"Is there a relationship in your life this brings to mind?"* Crucially, per the Paradigm document's own instruction for this stage: *"Do not correct. The associations themselves are material for the workshop."* If a participant offers an early interpretation ("I think the dragon represents anger"), the Workshop Plan's existing Recognition-support line applies here just as well: receive it without correcting, and return them to observation — *"What in the image led you there?"*

**What the participant does.** Free-associates aloud or silently. Danger, protection, power, destruction, courage, enemy, defender — the Paradigm document itself names this exact range as the expected spread, and none of it needs to be steered toward the "correct" reading.

**Existing material to use.** None directly — this content doesn't exist as a discrete asset anywhere in the current system. The Guide's rung-2 questions and the "receive without correcting" instruction (already written, just living in the Recognition-support section of the old plan) supply the technique.

**Material to move/reframe.** The old plan's "Recognition Support" line (*"Ask only what was noticed, not what it means... receive it without correcting, and return them to observation"*) belongs here, moved from where it currently sits (attached to the old plan's Recognition stage) to Association, where it actually describes the right posture.

**Genuinely new material.** A short bank of Association-stage prompts specific to Dragon, beyond the Guide's generic rung-2 questions — e.g., "If you had to give this figure a single word, what would it be?" This is a light lift, not a structural gap.

**Timing.** 7 minutes.

**Transition to Story/Poem.** Susan names the pivot honestly, without editorializing: "You've each brought something different to this already. Let's add one more thing to what you're seeing." The poem appears.

**Entertainment/engagement mechanism.** Participation and contrast. A room hearing five different readings of the same figure in five different words is, on its own, a small piece of theater — the Guide's own group-dynamics chapter treats this divergence as "one of the most valuable things a group format offers," not a problem to manage.

## Stage 4 — Story/Poem: Deepen the Encounter

**Purpose.** Let language complicate what participants thought they were seeing. The Paradigm document is explicit that this is where "AwakenArts' literary identity becomes integral to the instruction rather than ornamental."

**What the participant sees/hears.** The full Dragon poem, in its existing shaped, dragon-silhouette typographic form — title in gold/blue, body in blue serif with italicized clauses, closing couplet in orange: *"LIKE TWO SIDES OF A GOLDEN COIN, THE PARTS WERE MADE TO LIVE AS ONE."* Read aloud by Susan, slowly, or given a full minute of silent reading time before any discussion — the Guide's silence discipline applies to text exactly as it does to image.

**What Susan says/does.** Reads the poem, or invites a participant to read it, without commentary before or after. Then a single laddering question, staying at association/feeling rather than jumping to meaning: *"What happens in you when you sit with that?"*

**What the participant does.** Listens to language actively working against or alongside their stage-3 associations — the poem's own violence ("dragon claws scored dragon nails like metal rakes," "the steaming lightning bolts of hate") sits directly against its closing reconciliation image, without resolving the tension yet.

**Existing material to use.** The poem itself — fully produced, already the strongest asset in the system, and currently the org's own working copy of the full verbatim text does not exist in any legible source file; it survives only as artwork. **KEEP the content outright; this blueprint treats transcription as solved for planning purposes, but the authoritative text still needs Susan's own confirmation before it becomes slide copy** (see reserved decisions, below, and Section 3).

**Material to move/reframe.** None — the poem does not need reframing, only staging (reading aloud, held silence) rather than being handed to a reader as a printed page.

**Genuinely new material.** None for content. What's new is presentation: a spoken/performed reading rather than a page turn.

**Timing.** 10 minutes.

**Transition to Disruption.** No summary of the poem's meaning. Susan simply says: "Hold what you just heard. We're going to look at this figure one more time." The same image returns, but the frame has shifted — this is the workshop's first "image returning with altered significance" beat, in miniature, previewing the full Return at stage 10.

**Entertainment/engagement mechanism.** Story, poetry, and juxtaposition. This stage is close to pure craft — its entertainment value is the poem's own violence-into-reconciliation arc, already built, already strong; nothing needs to be added on top of it.

## Stage 5 — Disruption: Not This or That, But And

**Purpose.** Stage the workshop's central discovery — the dragon's fire lands on the town it believes it's defending — as a live reveal, not a stated thesis. This is Dragon's single strongest asset. Goal 1 flagged that the Edition currently states "Not This or That, But And" as a Cover-page thesis *before* the discovery happens; the New Paradigm's own point 4 recommends moving it to a late reveal instead.

**What the participant sees/hears.** A single new piece of information, delivered as reveal rather than footnote: the dragon in this story is not fighting an external enemy — its own fire is what's landing on the town. If a title-card version of "Not This or That, But And" has appeared anywhere earlier in the session (a cover slide, a program handout), this is the one and only content adjustment this blueprint recommends making to the *existing* Edition material: hold that phrase back until this moment.

**What Susan says/does.** States the disruption plainly and briefly — this is not a moment for a laddering question, it's a moment for a clean fact that recontextualizes everything before it: "The dragon believes it's defending the town. Its own fire is what's falling on it." Then stops. No interpretation offered yet.

**What the participant does.** Sits with the recontextualization. This is the first moment the workshop deliberately asks something uncomfortable of the room — consistent with the readiness-before-depth principle, this is minute 30–40, not minute 5.

**Existing material to use.** The dragon's own story mechanism — Goal 1's #1-ranked preserved asset. **KEEP the mechanism outright.**

**Material to move/reframe.** The Companion's Learning subsection "A Battle the Dragon Did Not Choose" contains the fully-written version of this exact insight (*"He is certain of his cause, and blind only to his own reflection — the fire he breathes lands on the very town he believes he is defending"*) — currently written as essay prose meant to be read. **REFRAME**: this becomes the intellectual outline for what Susan says live, not text anyone reads on a page. The essay's own closing question — *"Where else have you fought a battle for a long time without recognizing whom you were actually fighting?"* — is strong enough to move forward almost unchanged into stage 7 (Recognition), not asked here.

**Genuinely new material.** A short, precise script for the reveal itself — one or two sentences, delivered live, timed to land after the poem and before any explanation. This does not exist as spoken content anywhere in the current system; it exists only as the essay's opening paragraphs.

**Timing.** 10 minutes (includes the silence that should follow the reveal — this is exactly the kind of moment the Guide warns facilitators rush to fill: *"Resist it anyway. Silence after a real question is usually not emptiness."* The same applies to silence after a real disclosure).

**Transition to Meta Shift.** After the silence settles, Susan turns the room's attention from the dragon to itself: "Before we talk about what this means — I want to ask what just happened in you when I said that."

**Entertainment/engagement mechanism.** Surprise, reveal, and the "ambiguity followed by recognition" tool named explicitly in the Paradigm document's own entertainment toolkit. This is the single moment in the whole arc built most directly to answer "would I want to keep watching this if nobody required me to?" — and it already exists, fully formed, in Dragon's own plot.

## Stage 6 — Meta Shift: Turn Attention Toward the Observer

**Purpose.** The explicit pivot from object level to meta level — the moment the participant experiences metacognition rather than hearing it defined. Goal 1 flagged this as one of two stages (with Application) that exist nowhere in the current system as written content.

**What the participant sees/hears.** No new image or text. The same dragon, the same room — but Susan's questions now point at the participant's own mind rather than at the figure.

**What Susan says/does.** The Paradigm document supplies this stage's questions almost verbatim, and they should be used close to as-written, since they're already precisely calibrated to avoid re-explaining Disruption instead of examining it: *"What happened in your thinking? What did you initially believe was happening? What information did you use? What did you supply? What changed when additional information appeared?"* These are meta-level in a way the *existing* Reflection questions are not — Goal 1's own analysis is explicit that the current Reflection questions ("Where Do I Notice Division Within Myself?") ask what the participant recognizes, not what their mind just did, and that distinction is the entire reason this stage needs its own content rather than reusing Reflection early.

**What the participant does.** Names, out loud or in writing, the gap between their initial read and the reveal — not what the dragon means, but what their own mind assumed, filled in, or missed.

**Existing material to use.** None directly as written content. The object/meta distinction itself, fully defined in the Paradigm document, supplies the conceptual frame; nothing in the Edition or Companion currently operationalizes it as a question sequence.

**Material to move/reframe.** None to move — there is no prior version of this content to reframe. This is the cleanest "genuinely new" stage in the entire arc.

**Genuinely new material.** The full Meta Shift question set. The Paradigm document's own five questions above are strong enough to use directly; what's missing is Dragon-specific framing language connecting them to the town/fire reveal just experienced, and facilitator guidance for what to do with the range of answers a room will give (some participants will answer easily; others will need the Guide's rung-3 fallback — *"What happens in you when you sit with that?"* — if the more abstract "what did your mind do" question doesn't land).

**Timing.** 10 minutes.

**Transition to Recognition.** Susan bridges from the participant's own mind back out toward their life, using the Paradigm document's own transition language nearly verbatim: "Now — where else has this happened?"

**Entertainment/engagement mechanism.** Recognition itself, as the Paradigm document names it directly: *"This is where the participant experiences metacognition rather than merely hearing a definition of it."* The craft here is restraint — resisting the urge to explain the concept of metacognition, letting the questions alone do the work. This is also the stage where the self-preparation note in the Facilitator Notes is most load-bearing: *"Facilitators who arrive without having faced their own 'and' tend to over-explain in the room."*

## Stage 7 — Recognition: Where Does This Touch My Experience?

**Purpose.** Move carefully from Dragon to life. The question shifts, per the Paradigm document, from "What does Dragon mean?" to "What did this encounter allow me to recognize?"

**What the participant sees/hears.** The Larger Story material — Scripture and the Christian tradition — introduced here, after the meta-level turn has already happened rather than before it (see Reserved Decision 4, below, on Scripture placement). Current text: *"'A double minded man is unstable in all his ways.' — James 1:8 (KJV)"* alongside Augustine's *Confessions* on the divided will.

**What Susan says/does.** Introduces the Larger Story briefly, then returns to the existing Reflection questions — reframed, per Goal 1's own recommendation, as this stage's content rather than Meta Shift's. Current production text: *"Where Do I Notice Division Within Myself?"*, *"Where Does the Voice of Judgment Appear?"*, *"What Am I Still Trying to Conquer?"*, and the "And" question in its current form. These are strong, personally relevant, object-adjacent-but-reaching-toward-meta questions — exactly right for this stage, wrong only if placed earlier.

**What the participant does.** Answers privately (written or silent) before any group sharing — the Guide's individual-reflection discipline protects this from becoming performative.

**Existing material to use.** The Edition's full Reflection sequence — **REFRAME, not rewrite**: same questions, new position in the arc (moved later than their current placement, since in the Edition they currently sit before Journal with no explicit Meta Shift beat ahead of them).

**Material to move/reframe.** The Companion's "Rage as a Revealer, Not an Enemy" essay belongs here, reframed into a brief spoken transition rather than read prose — its closing question ("What have you called rage, when it may have been an unmet longing to protect something that matters to you?") is close to ready-made Recognition content.

**Genuinely new material.** A short bridging line connecting the Meta Shift's abstract "what did my mind do" answers to this stage's more personal "where does this show up in my life" — currently there is a hard cut between the two registers in the existing material; this blueprint is the first place that cut gets named as something to smooth, not solved yet.

**Timing.** 12 minutes.

**Transition to Conscious Language.** Susan closes discussion, if any was opened aloud, with the old plan's own instruction, still sound: return to the image, allow thirty seconds of silence, then move forward. "Let's put what you're noticing into words."

**Entertainment/engagement mechanism.** Personal relevance and recognition — the entertainment tool here is not spectacle but stakes: the moment the workshop stops being about a dragon and starts being about the participant's own life is, done well, the most gripping turn in the whole arc, not the quietest one.

## Stage 8 — Conscious Language: Write It

**Purpose.** Let recognition become language. The Paradigm document is precise about why this matters and is not merely ceremonial: *"Something felt or partially perceived becomes: 'This is what I am beginning to see.' Once expressed in language, it becomes something the participant can examine."*

**What the participant sees/hears.** Minimal — writing space, quiet, perhaps the image held on screen without further comment. This is the arc's second deliberately quiet stage (after Encounter's opening silence).

**What Susan says/does.** Very little. The existing Journal page language, current production text, already does this well and needs no rewriting: *"Choose one recognition from this Edition to carry with you today. You do not need to resolve it. You do not need to fix it. Simply notice what returns to your attention as you move through ordinary life."*

**What the participant does.** Writes, privately, for an extended uninterrupted stretch — this is the stage the old Workshop Plan protected as the single longest individual stage in both its 60- and 90-minute formats, and this blueprint preserves that discipline for the same reason: recognition needs room, not prompting, to become language.

**Existing material to use.** The Journal page — **KEEP as-is**, per Goal 1's own finding. No changes recommended.

**Material to move/reframe.** None needed.

**Genuinely new material.** None. This is the one stage in the entire arc that is already fully solved by existing material.

**Timing.** 12 minutes.

**Transition to Application.** Susan gives a soft time-warning at the two-minute mark, then closes with a single line, not a question: "When you're ready, come back to the room."

**Entertainment/engagement mechanism.** None deliberately added, and none needed — this is a stage where the craft is knowing not to entertain. Sustained quiet after five stages of active engagement is itself a pacing device; a workshop that never slows down is exhausting, not entertaining.

## Stage 9 — Application: What Do I Want to Do With This?

**Purpose.** Do not end on recognition alone. This is the arc's other confirmed content gap — Goal 1 flagged that the Edition currently ends on Living the Message "without an explicit Application beat," closing on recognition and image rather than a forward-looking choice.

**What the participant sees/hears.** Susan asking a small, direct set of questions, not delivering more content. No new imagery.

**What Susan says/does.** The Paradigm document supplies this stage's question set directly and it is strong as-is: *"What did I recognize? Where does this appear in my life? What becomes different when I can see it? Is this something I want to continue carrying forward? What choice becomes available to me now?"* The old Workshop Plan's Integration stage already contains two ready-made, lightweight closing practices worth folding in here rather than reinventing: **"Name the Two"** (identify one pair of things in your life you've been treating as opposites) and **"Carry the Word"** (notice, in the coming week, where "and" might apply where "or" has been assumed).

**What the participant does.** Chooses — the Paradigm document is explicit that "the participant determines the answer," and Susan's role here is to ask, not to suggest what the choice should be.

**Existing material to use.** The old Workshop Plan's "Name the Two" and "Carry the Word" practices — **MOVE and reuse directly**, lightly relabeled as Application-stage content rather than Integration.

**Material to move/reframe.** Living the Message's existing closing questions (*"Where have you begun to notice this pattern in your everyday life? What changes when you replace judgment with attention?"*) are close cousins of true Application questions and can be folded in rather than duplicated — see Stage 10, where Living the Message's content actually belongs.

**Genuinely new material.** A brief bridging script connecting the Paradigm's five Application questions to the two existing practices, so the stage doesn't read as two unrelated activities stacked together.

**Timing.** 8 minutes.

**Transition to Return.** No summary. Susan says: "Let's go back to where we started."

**Entertainment/engagement mechanism.** Agency. The Paradigm document's own quality-standard checklist names this directly: *"Are we leaving the conclusion with the participant rather than telling her what her recognition should mean?"* The entertainment value here is the same one a good ending always has — the sense that something is genuinely, personally at stake in what happens next, not that the workshop is wrapping up a lesson.

## Stage 10 — Return: Complete the Experience

**Purpose.** Close the loop. The Paradigm document: *"Return visually or verbally to something from the beginning... The image has not necessarily changed. The observer has more information with which to see it."*

**What the participant sees/hears.** The same dragon image from Encounter, shown one final time, now alongside the poem's closing couplet: *"LIKE TWO SIDES OF A GOLDEN COIN, THE PARTS WERE MADE TO LIVE AS ONE."*

**What Susan says/does.** Closes with the existing Message Delivered and Living the Message content, current production text — this is strong, finished material and needs only light reframing from "reading page" register to "closing spoken word" register: *"The parts were never meant to compete. They were made to live as one. Not this or that. But and."* The old Workshop Plan's own closing line still works as a final spoken beat: *"Not this or that — but and."* Followed by a moment of silence before ending the session, per the plan's own instruction.

**What the participant does.** Receives the closing without being asked to do anything further — this is the one stage after Orientation that asks nothing new of the participant, by design, mirroring Orientation's own restraint.

**Existing material to use.** Message Delivered and Living the Message — **KEEP, lightly reframed** as a closing beat rather than a reading page, per Goal 1's own recommendation.

**Material to move/reframe.** None beyond the register shift already noted.

**Genuinely new material.** None for content. What's new is the deliberate visual return — showing the *same* image from Encounter rather than new closing artwork — which doesn't currently happen anywhere in the Edition's linear reading structure, since a printed book doesn't have to manage live pacing across 90 minutes the way a room does.

**Timing.** 8 minutes.

**Transition out.** None needed — this is the arc's last stage. Susan ends the session in the silence, without a debrief or wrap-up announcement layered on top of it.

**Entertainment/engagement mechanism.** "An image returning later with altered significance" — named directly in the Paradigm document's own toolkit, and this is its fullest expression in the whole arc: the same picture, unchanged, now seen by a room that has spent ninety minutes changing.

## Where the Reserved Decisions Touch This Blueprint

Per Susan's instruction, none of these are resolved here. Each is shown at the exact point it affects the participant experience, with the strongest viable alternatives and a stated recommendation.

### 1. James 4:7 ("resist the devil, and he will flee from you") vs. "not a story about defeating a monster"

**Where it touches the blueprint.** Not directly, as currently designed — the workshop's own Larger Story (Stage 7) already uses James 1:8 and Augustine, not James 4:7, and the entire arc is built around reconciliation ("the parts were made to live as one"), not conquest. The James 4:7 conflict lives specifically on the separate `/encounters/dragon` website page, which this blueprint does not touch and does not assume as a workshop entry point.

**Why it still matters here.** If `/encounters/dragon` is ever connected into the visitor journey leading toward this workshop — which Goal 1 flagged as a plausible future move — a participant could arrive having just read an adversarial epigraph, then sit through ninety minutes explicitly built around *not* framing the dragon as an enemy to defeat. ## For Susan as a Novice-but-Expert Presenter

A few threads that don't belong to any single stage, gathered here because a first-time presenter needs them held in one place rather than scattered across ten sections:

**Opening language is now written** (Stage 1) — this was the single largest gap standing between the current material and something a presenter could pick up and run without inventing her own words in the room.

**Silence is the technique most likely to feel wrong while being right.** Three separate stages (Encounter, Disruption, Conscious Language) depend on silences most facilitators find uncomfortably long — a full minute or more at Encounter, a real pause after the Disruption reveal, and a full uninterrupted writing stretch at Conscious Language. The Guide's own diagnostic is worth carrying into the room verbatim: productive silence looks like stillness; stalled silence looks like restlessness and clock-checking. Only the second kind needs a bridge, and the bridge is small — *"Where are you right now?"* — not a rescue.

**Likely stall points and recovery.** If a participant deflects or intellectualizes early (most likely at Association or Recognition), the Guide's resistance framing applies directly: name it without judgment, and hand back a real choice — *"It sounds like this one isn't landing today — do you want to stay with it, or set it aside?"* If one participant's reading threatens to become the room's reading (most likely after Disruption, when one strong response can dominate), the Guide's redirect line is ready to use as-is: *"That's a powerful read for you — I want to make sure we leave room for everyone else's encounter to be just as much its own."*

**Where to resist over-explaining.** Three moments carry the highest risk of Susan doing the work the questions are supposed to do: immediately after the Disruption reveal (the temptation to explain what it means rather than ask what happened), during Meta Shift (the temptation to define metacognition rather than let the questions produce it), and anywhere Learning content has been reframed into spoken material (the temptation to deliver the essay rather than the outline of it). The Facilitator Notes' own closing line is the sharpest available check: facilitators who haven't faced their own "and" tend to over-explain in the room — worth Susan's own private reflection before a first run, not just a note in this document.

**What a novice presenter does not need to invent.** Every question asked across all ten stages above is either already written somewhere in the existing system or drawn directly from the Guide's own laddering technique. Nothing in this blueprint asks Susan to write live facilitation language from scratch in the room.
