## What an AwakenArts Figure Edition Is

> An AwakenArts Figure Edition does not seek to explain experience. It offers a symbolic shape through which readers may recognize patterns already present in ordinary life.

An AwakenArts Figure Edition is not primarily an art book, a poetry collection, or a workbook. It combines image, symbolic language, and guided recognition to help readers discover meaningful patterns within ordinary life.

The edition is intended to create recognition rather than explanation. *Shape* is the operative word, not incidentally: it is the same word at the center of AwakenArts' own tagline, "When Language Shapes a Path." An edition gives a reader a shape for something they had already sensed but never named — the shape is what they carry out, not an explanation of it.

### The Figure Edition Series Statement

This defines the series, not any single edition — the answer to the question a reader, retailer, or workshop leader actually asks: *what is a Figure Edition, in one sentence?*

> Each Figure Edition invites readers into a symbolic story that becomes a way of recognizing the patterns that shape ordinary life.

This is permanent AwakenArts publishing language, not promotional copy — the defining description of what distinguishes a Figure Edition from an art book, a poetry collection, a workbook, or a study guide. Use it, worded consistently, wherever a Figure Edition is introduced to someone encountering the series for the first time: the website's Collection page, the Figure Edition Series Introduction, an edition's own description, Leader's Resource openings, and any future marketing material. It should read the same way everywhere, because it is naming one thing, not summarizing several. The wording holds unless a future editorial revision intentionally replaces it.

**Why this replaces ingredients-first language.** Earlier descriptions led with what AwakenArts makes — "combines image, poetry, symbolic language..." That is accurate but sells the wrong thing. A reader does not buy image-plus-poetry-plus-questions; they buy the experience those ingredients produce, and the change that experience makes possible. This statement leads with what the reader receives, not with what went into making it. Ingredient language still belongs in production documentation, where precision about components matters. It does not belong in the sentence that introduces the series to a new reader.

### Series Identity and Individual Voice

Every AwakenArts Figure Edition shares a common editorial architecture, reader journey, and publishing identity. Within that shared framework, each edition is expected to develop its own symbolic voice, its own conversation with Scripture and the Christian tradition, and its own distinctive literary character. Consistency is found in the series' rhythm and methodology, not in repeating the same themes, authors, or conclusions. The result should be a body of work that is immediately recognizable as AwakenArts while allowing each figure to speak in its own way.

## Recognition, Not Interpretation — The Parable Principle

AwakenArts Figure Editions are not designed to teach symbolic interpretation. They are designed to cultivate recognition. Like a parable, the value of the encounter is not exhausted when the book is closed — it is discovered afterward, as the reader begins recognizing the same pattern within ordinary life.

Until now, an edition's implicit question has been *"What does this symbol mean?"* Every edition going forward asks instead: *"Where have you begun to see this pattern in your own life?"*

That is a shift from symbolic interpretation to a continuing experience. It anchors AwakenArts to the way parables work: they don't merely explain — they reshape perception, and over time that new way of seeing reshapes life. Jesus rarely ended a parable with an explanation; he left people looking at ordinary life differently. That is the model, not exposition of a lesson.

### The Bridge Principle

The editorial principle, stated at the level it actually governs:

> Every edition should bridge the symbolic encounter to ordinary life.

This is bigger than any single question — it is what the closing movement of every Figure Edition exists to do. Dragon's own revision produced one instance of it:

> Where have you begun to notice this pattern in ordinary life?

That sentence does the actual work of a parable: it takes the reader from the symbolic world back into everyday experience, without explaining what they should find there. It is the working example of the Bridge Principle, not the principle itself — a future edition may build its own bridge in different words, so long as it performs the same function: not asking the reader what the symbol means, but where they have already, on their own, begun to notice it. The other closing questions (Standard: "Living the Message," Production Standard Section 2) are more freely adaptable per edition; the bridge itself — in whatever words a given edition finds — is not optional.

The reason this question works without specialized vocabulary is itself a governing example: Dragon's early language ("integrating the shadow") required a reader to already know Jungian psychology to feel invited in. Recast into ordinary experience — strength and tenderness, sun and moon, action and reflection, not this or that but and — the same recognition becomes available to a reader with no framework at all. A church group, a counselor, a book club, and an individual with no background in any of it can all enter from where they stand. This is what "recognition rather than explanation" is meant to produce in practice, and it should be treated as the test for an edition's language generally, not only for this one question.

## The Reader Journey

Every Figure Edition moves the reader through the same psychological arc, regardless of how many pages that takes or how any individual figure expresses it visually:

**Encounter → Notice → Recognize → Reflect → Respond → Return to Ordinary Life**

- **Encounter** — the reader meets the figure and the poem before anything is explained. Atmosphere and image do the work; nothing is glossed.
- **Notice** — the reader's own affective response joins the image as material worth attending to, before interpretation begins.
- **Recognize** — the symbolic pattern comes into focus. What looked like two things reveals itself as one.
- **Reflect** — the reader turns the recognition toward their own life, privately.
- **Respond** — the reader writes, sits with, or otherwise metabolizes what surfaced.
- **Return to Ordinary Life** — the edition closes not with a conclusion, but with an open invitation to keep noticing the same pattern after the book is shut.

This describes the reader's psychological movement, not a fixed page count or page order. A shorter or longer edition can honor the same arc.

## The Governing Principle of Usefulness

Every Figure Edition should leave the reader with something they can recognize, reflect upon, and use in the ordinary movements of life.

The objective is not explanation. The objective is recognition that becomes useful in ordinary life. Every Figure Edition should help readers notice something they had not previously recognized and carry that recognition into ordinary life.

This principle governs the closing movement of every edition (see the Production Standard's page-sequence section) but is not limited to it — it is a test that can be applied to any editorial decision, front to back.

## Preserve Mystery

Symbolic interpretation should illuminate without exhausting meaning. Recognition pages should deepen understanding while preserving room for continued discovery. The work should never become overly explanatory.

An edition that fully explains its own symbol has finished its job too early — nothing is left for the reader to keep discovering after the reading. Restraint in explanation is not a lesser version of clarity; it is what allows an edition to keep working on the reader over time.

## Two Publications: Edition and Companion

An AwakenArts Figure Edition serves the reader having the encounter. It is not a facilitator manual, and it should never be asked to do a facilitator manual's job.

**Governing naming standard, 2026-07-07.** Every symbolic figure now consists of two companion publications, named consistently across the series: the **[Figure] Edition** (e.g., *Dragon Edition*, *Grismere Edition*, *Bowls Edition*, *Queen Ann Edition*) and the **[Figure] Companion** (e.g., *Dragon Companion*). This replaces "Leader's Resource" as the series-wide term; facilitator guidance is one thing the Companion provides, not the whole of what it is.

**[Figure] Edition** — purpose: symbolic encounter, personal recognition, reflection, journaling, continued awareness. This is the primary publication, and the literary work intended for participants and readers. This is the product a reader buys for themselves, a gift-giver buys for someone else, or a small group of friends reads together without anyone directing the room.

**[Figure] Companion** — purpose: to deepen understanding of the Edition without interrupting its literary integrity. This is where facilitator guidance, teaching cautions, discussion sequencing, group application, author notes, and the background thinking behind the figure all live. It is not another edition, and it must never duplicate the participant experience — a reader who wants only the Edition should never feel they missed something essential; a reader who wants more should find it here, deepened rather than repeated.

These are separate publications serving different needs. Facilitator material — timing, scripts, teaching cautions, discussion sequencing — belongs exclusively to the Companion. It is removed entirely from the Edition, not merely reduced.

This produces a natural product ladder: an individual buys the Edition; a family, book club, or small group uses the Edition together, unassisted; a church, counselor, retreat leader, or teacher buys the Companion because they want a fuller experience to lead or a deeper one to keep. Each level has a clear reason to exist, and none of them compete with each other.

**The governing sentence, stated once for the whole series:** *The [Figure] Edition is the encounter. The [Figure] Companion explains, supports, and facilitates the encounter without becoming the encounter itself.* Concretely, for the founding edition: **the Dragon Edition is the encounter; the Dragon Companion explains, supports, and facilitates the encounter without becoming the encounter itself.** This sentence exists to prevent the Companion from drifting into a second edition — everything it contains serves the Edition; nothing in it replaces having the Edition.

**The Companion's internal architecture** — Foundation, Interpretation, Engagement, Application — was discovered through the Dragon Companion recovery process and is now a permanent series standard. See `AwakenArts_Figure_Edition_Production_Standard.md`, Section 1a, for the full structure and governing notes.

**A statement of identity, not a production rule.** The Edition invites the encounter. The Companion preserves the conversation behind the encounter. Together they allow the symbolic figure to speak with literary integrity while providing thoughtful guidance for those who wish to explore it more deeply.

## The Christian Story: Where Recognition Finds Its Fulfillment

**The theological center of the entire series, named directly, 2026-07-06 — not one more principle alongside the others in this section, but the ground they all stand on.**

Across many weeks of refining layouts, questions, typography, production standards, and editorial voice, none of that work named this directly. It should be named directly now, because everything else in this document — and every Figure Edition — organizes itself around it once it is:

The central truth of AwakenArts is not "know yourself," not "integrate your shadow," not even "become whole." Those are real and important, but they are not the Gospel. **The Gospel is that God has acted in Christ to reconcile what humanity could not reconcile for itself.** AwakenArts' symbolic figures help people recognize where that reconciliation is needed. They do not accomplish it. Christ does. That distinction protects both the integrity of the symbolic story and the integrity of the Christian faith — the figures are not the answer; they are what makes a reader ready to hear one.

> The purpose of every Figure Edition is not merely to awaken recognition but to prepare the imagination to receive the deeper truth revealed in the Gospel. Recognition is the doorway; Christ is the fulfillment.

This is why the Larger Story page exists at all — not to append Christian content to a finished edition, but because every edition is, from its foundation, deciding what AwakenArts ultimately bears witness to. The question every edition should quietly answer is not "What does this figure mean?" It is **"How does this figure prepare us to hear the Gospel more deeply?"** That is a higher calling than interpretation, and it is why Dragon's own Larger Story page took seven revisions before its underlying question — not merely its wording — was finally right: each revision was reaching for this center before it had been named.

**A caution this document takes seriously.** Layouts, questions, typography, production standards, and editorial voice can all be revised freely, and have been, repeatedly, throughout this project. This paragraph is different in kind, not merely in importance — it is the theological foundation the rest of the series stands on. It should not be revised at the same pace as everything else in this document, or by the same casual editorial process.

### Recognition and Truth

The editorial test beneath every other test in this document is not, "Does this sound Christian?" It is, "Is this true?" Only then do we ask, "Is it true in a way that is faithful to Christ and the witness of Scripture?"

This is a higher and more demanding standard than vocabulary or tone because it cannot be satisfied by Christian language alone.

Recognition has always been the governing language of AwakenArts because a symbolic story cannot awaken recognition of what is not there. It does not create truth; it bears witness to it. Its purpose is to help readers perceive more clearly something that is genuinely true about the human condition.

For the Christian, however, recognition is never the final authority. Jesus Christ is "the way, the truth, and the life" (John 14:6). Scripture uniquely bears authoritative witness to Him and provides the measure by which every symbolic insight is tested. Creation, human experience, and symbolic stories may illuminate that same reality, but they remain accountable to the truth God has revealed.

An AwakenArts Figure Edition has integrity when its symbolic recognition is found to be faithful to that reality. It does not borrow the authority of Scripture; nor does it stand alongside Scripture. Rather, it seeks to bear honest literary witness to the same human condition that Scripture reveals and that Christ ultimately answers.

This protects AwakenArts from two opposite errors: symbolic psychology that borrows Christian language without accountability to Christ, and Christian symbolism that speaks Christian vocabulary without truthfully engaging the realities of human experience.

Every Figure Edition is therefore an act of recognition twice over: first, recognizing what is genuinely true about human life; second, receiving that recognition within the larger truth revealed by God in Christ.

### The Vocation of a Figure Edition

Before this methodology was named, an AwakenArts Figure Edition could be described by what it looked like — image, poem, guided reflection, now also Scripture and the Christian tradition. It can now be described by what it is for. Its purpose is not merely to present beautiful symbolic art. It is not merely to stimulate discussion. It is not merely to teach Christian doctrine. **Its purpose is to prepare the imagination** — to help a reader recognize, through a figure they could not easily approach directly, something they could not easily receive directly either. That preparation is not manipulation, and it is not allegory. It is pedagogy in the deepest sense: the same movement Jesus used in his own parables, working through what is recognizable toward what is revealed.

This is why a Figure Edition is best understood as a modern literary parable rather than a devotional or a study guide — not a biblical parable, a claim AwakenArts does not make, but a work sharing a biblical parable's educational movement: begin with something recognizable; invite sustained reflection; reveal something deeper; receive that insight into the larger truth of God's revelation; invite faithful living. See "Recognition, Not Interpretation — The Parable Principle," above, for the literary mechanics this vocation grows out of.

**The dual standard every edition must satisfy.** Because a Figure Edition is two things at once — a complete literary work, and a work that receives its own recognition into the truth revealed in Christ — it is judged on two independent levels, and both must pass:

1. Does the edition stand as a complete literary work on its own? If a reader stopped before reaching The Larger Story, everything before it should still possess full artistic integrity — image, poem, and guided reflection that need nothing added to be whole as literature.
2. Does The Larger Story faithfully receive that literary work into the truth revealed in Christ, tested against "Recognition and Truth," above?

If the answer to either question is no, the edition is not finished — regardless of how well it satisfies the other. This dual standard is one of AwakenArts' defining, structural characteristics, not an incidental byproduct of adding a Christian Story page to an already-complete work.

**Why Dragon needed seven revisions, stated for the record.** Dragon was not fixed seven times. It was the edition that discovered this method. The vocation above, the dual standard above, and the corrected Scripture-selection method recorded in `AwakenArts_Figure_Edition_Production_Standard.md`, Section 11, all emerged from working through Dragon specifically — they were not a pre-existing rule applied to it. Every future edition now begins from a mature editorial philosophy, production standard, theological methodology, and literary rhythm that Dragon had to discover by trial and revision, which is why future editions should need fewer revisions to reach the same place. Fewer is not the same as none.

**Discovered, not learned — the five-step progression of truth across an edition.** A word-level distinction settled late in Dragon's revision history turned out to name something structural: the symbolic figure *discovers* truth already there, it does not *learn* something the struggle itself creates. Dragon's own closing page was corrected on exactly this point — "brings a message discovered through struggle" rather than "learned through struggle" — because "learned" suggests the struggle produced the truth, while "discovered" says the struggle uncovered what had been true all along. This word choice sits inside a five-step progression that now governs how truth moves through every Figure Edition: the truth already exists; the symbolic figure discovers it; the reader recognizes it; Scripture names it; Christ reconciles it. No step is optional and no step does another step's job — the figure that discovers is never the Scripture that names, and recognizing is never the same act as reconciling.

**The complete literary rhythm — six pages, six distinct responsibilities.** Once Dragon's full revision arc closed, its six central pages were found to divide the edition's entire movement without overlap: the poem awakens recognition; Recognition names what is true; Reflect invites personal engagement without prematurely resolving it; The Larger Story receives that recognition into the Christian story; the edition's closing symbolic statement (Dragon's "Message Delivered") expresses what has been discovered; and the closing movement (Dragon's "Living the Message") sends the reader back into ordinary life without closing the conversation. Each page's job is to do only its own part of this rhythm — a page that tries to do another page's work (Recognition explaining Scripture, Reflect resolving what only Christ reconciles, the closing movement summarizing the whole edition rather than releasing the reader into their own life) has stepped outside its role, regardless of how well-written the sentence itself is. This rhythm, not any one edition's specific wording, is what every future Figure Edition is built to reproduce.

**The sentence that governs why every Figure Edition exists, not only how this lens is applied:**

> Each Figure Edition invites readers into a symbolic story that becomes a way of recognizing patterns that shape ordinary life. Those patterns ultimately find their fullest meaning within the larger story of God's redemptive work in Christ.

This is not a statement about symbolism, and it is not a statement about Scripture. It is a statement about purpose — why AwakenArts publishes these editions at all. Every principle below serves this one sentence; none of them replace it.

Everything above this section was built through symbolic, literary, psychological, and experiential recognition. That is the foundation, and it is not being replaced. This section completes it with one more developmental lens, present from the earliest stages of every edition going forward — not something added at the end, during proofreading:

> How does this symbolic story find its fulfillment within the Christian story?

**Why this movement exists.** Throughout Scripture, God frequently teaches through story, parable, poetry, metaphor, symbol, and lived experience. Jesus did not use stories simply because they were memorable — he used them because they opened the heart to recognize truths about the Kingdom of God. AwakenArts has adopted that same literary approach. The symbolic figure is therefore not presented as an independent source of truth. The symbolic story prepares the reader to recognize truths that Scripture has been revealing all along. The Christian story is not an appendix to the symbolic story. It is its fulfillment.

**What this is not.** The Christian story is not another section of the edition. It is not a separate lesson. It is not a doctrinal appendix. It is not Christian content attached to an otherwise complete edition — an edition is not "complete" without it and then finished off by adding Scripture on top; the symbolic story and the Christian story do different work from the start, and neither is optional decoration on the other.

**What this is.** It is the larger narrative within which the symbolic story ultimately finds its deepest meaning. A Figure Edition remains literary, symbolic, and invitational — and is now also intentionally consistent with historic Christian doctrine.

**What the symbolic figure is not, stated as plainly as possible.** The figure is never the source of wisdom. The figure is never a parallel gospel. The figure is never a biblical character. The figure is a symbolic witness that prepares the reader to hear the Christian story with fresh eyes — nothing more, and this is not a diminishment of the figure's importance. Awakening that fresh-eyed readiness is real, necessary work; it is simply not the same work Scripture and the Christian tradition do next.

**The review questions.** Beginning with Dragon and continuing through every existing and future Figure Edition, development and revision ask:

1. What human pattern is this edition revealing?
2. What truth is the reader being invited to recognize?
3. What aspect of the Gospel does this recognition prepare the reader to receive?
4. How does this movement participate in the larger Christian story?
5. Does the edition remain faithful both to the symbolic story and to the Christian understanding of transformation?

*Question 3 above was corrected 2026-07-06.* It previously read "Where does this recognition naturally resonate with Scripture?" — see "The purpose of The Larger Story is theological, not a Scripture search," below, for why even that careful phrasing still started in the wrong place.

The goal is not to insert biblical references wherever possible. The goal is to let the symbolic story and the Christian story illuminate each other naturally.

**The purpose of The Larger Story is theological, not a Scripture search.** The symbolic figure already accomplishes its own purpose: it awakens recognition. The Larger Story exists to answer the question the figure raises — not to explain the figure, not to prove the figure with Scripture, and not to find a biblical story that resembles the figure. Dragon's own page went through six revisions because this distinction had not yet been made explicit, and each revision was correcting symptoms of the same underlying confusion rather than the confusion itself. **The symbolic figure is not the Gospel. It prepares the heart to receive the Gospel.** That distinction now governs every editorial decision on this page, for every edition.

The corrected method, in strict order — this is an editorial and theological exercise first, a Scripture-selection exercise only after:

1. Stop searching for passages that resemble the symbolic story, and stop searching for stories that parallel it. Resemblance — whether of vocabulary or of narrative shape — is not the test.
2. Identify the central Christian truth, the aspect of the Gospel, that the symbolic recognition prepares the reader to receive. Do this before naming any passage.
3. Only then determine whether Scripture, the Christian tradition, and the reader's response naturally grow from that truth. If no passage naturally fulfills the movement, keep searching — do not force a text because it shares vocabulary or narrative shape.

The question this method asks, from now on: not *"What Scripture fits the symbolic story?"* but **"What aspect of the Gospel does the symbolic story prepare the reader to receive?"**

**The governing principle.** The symbolic story remains the reader's first experience. The Christian story provides its deepest fulfillment. Neither competes with the other — each should strengthen the reader's understanding of the other.

**Scripture is the center of gravity, not a commentary on the symbolic story.** The reader should feel that the symbolic story prepared them to hear Scripture — not that the symbolic story is being explained by Scripture. A Figure Edition never claims to enact or fulfill Scripture in its own right; it gives symbolic form to a human struggle that Scripture reveals within the larger story of God's redemptive work. That distinction protects Scripture's authority while preserving the integrity of the symbolic story. Any page that brings the two into contact — "The Larger Story" above all — should be reviewed against this, not just against whether the Scripture chosen is a good fit.

> The symbolic story prepares the reader to recognize; Scripture reveals; the Christian tradition helps us understand; Christ remains the center.

This one sentence governs every application of this lens, on every edition, going forward. Susan later extended it by one further step, naming the movement's actual close — the sentence above is not superseded, only completed:

* The symbolic story awakens recognition.
* Scripture reveals God's truth.
* The Christian tradition helps illuminate that truth.
* Christ remains the center.
* The reader is invited to live in that reality.

This five-part movement is the editorial architecture of every Figure Edition's Larger Story page, and of this lens generally. The first four steps are the four voices named in "Sources of Interpretation" below; the fifth step — the reader invited to live in that reality — is why none of the first four are complete on their own, and why this page's closing paragraph always hands the movement to the reader rather than ending on the symbolic figure or on Scripture alone.

**Scripture may be cited or may remain implicit — this is a literary choice, not a fixed rule.** Scripture may be explicitly cited or may remain implicitly present, depending on what best serves the literary movement of the edition. The goal is faithful witness rather than uniform presentation. Some editions, or some pages within an edition, will be stronger for a quoted passage named outright (The Larger Story, Production Standard Section 2, is built to carry that weight). Others will be stronger with Scripture quietly shaping the language without being named at all — the way Recognition's own closing line ("received before it is lived") carries the logic of grace without the word "grace" ever appearing. Neither approach is more faithful than the other; each is judged by whether it serves the edition's literary movement.

**The editorial question this actually requires.** The discipline this lens asks for is not "how do I add Christian content to this page" — that question produces insertion, decoration, appendix. The right question is "where does the language itself already begin to carry the Christian story, and where does it need one true word to finish carrying it." Recognition's addition is the working example: it does not announce "now we are talking about grace." It simply lets the sentence's own logic — wholeness is received before it is lived, not self-made — say what grace says, in the edition's own voice. An edition worked at this level ends up unmistakably Christian without becoming didactic; an edition worked at the level of "which verse fits here" tends toward the opposite.

**How this gets documented.** As each edition is reviewed or revised under this lens, where the Christian story deepens, clarifies, or completes the symbolic movement should be recorded in that edition's own editorial record (see `Dragon_Editorial_Responses.md` for the existing pattern), so this becomes a consistent, visible discipline across the series rather than a private judgment call made anew each time.

**Where this lens becomes a page, not only a review.** Every Figure Edition now includes a required movement placed immediately after Recognition and before Reflection, where the symbolic recognition just established is explicitly brought into contact with the Christian story: the same human struggle the reader has just recognized through the symbolic figure has already been addressed within the biblical story. This placement lets Reflection respond to both the symbolic story and the Christian story, not only to the symbolism. See `AwakenArts_Figure_Edition_Production_Standard.md`, Section 2 and Section 11, for the mechanics; each figure will lead to different Scripture and different themes, but the movement itself — symbolic recognition, then its fulfillment made visible, then personal response — is now permanent across the series.

This section establishes the lens itself. It does not, on its own, interpret any single edition through it — that review happens edition by edition, starting with Dragon, as each is revisited.

The goal of AwakenArts is not simply to help readers recognize themselves in the symbolic story. Its goal is to help readers recognize that God's redemptive story speaks to the very same human realities. The symbolic story awakens recognition; the Christian story provides the larger narrative within which that recognition finds hope, meaning, and direction.

### Sources of Interpretation

**These four voices are not a sequence of commentary — they are a conversation among witnesses.** The symbolic figure bears witness through image and story. Scripture bears witness through God's revealed Word. The Christian tradition bears witness through those who have reflected on these same truths throughout the history of the Church. The reader is then invited to recognize that same movement within ordinary life. Each speaks in its own right, alongside the others — none of them explains another away, and none of them is merely illustrating what another already said.

Every Figure Edition is developed through four complementary voices, not one:

1. **The symbolic story** — the literary encounter. It prepares the imagination, giving a human struggle shape and image before anything is explained.
2. **Scripture** — the primary authority. It is the authoritative witness the symbolic story ultimately answers to, not one perspective among several. **The biblical passage is selected because it shares the same redemptive movement as the symbolic story, not merely similar vocabulary or themes.** (See `AwakenArts_Figure_Edition_Production_Standard.md`, Section 2, and `Dragon_Christian_Tradition_Research.md` for the method this standard was learned from — moving from Ephesians 2:14, selected for matching words, to Luke 8, selected for matching narrative shape.) **Narrative shape is necessary but not sufficient — the passage must also share the symbolic story's theological center of gravity.** Dragon's own history demonstrates the gap: Luke 8's Legion narrative rhymed with Dragon's plot (many made one, restored, sent as messenger) closely enough to survive a full research pass, yet it is a story of deliverance from an external, invading force — the wrong center of gravity for a story about one person's own opposing parts needing reconciliation. It was replaced with Romans 7:15 and 8:1–2, matched not on plot but on the actual question the symbolic story is asking: *what biblical truth makes reconciliation of the story's opposing parts possible?*
3. **Voices from the Christian tradition** — Church Fathers, Reformers, theologians, spiritual writers, Christian philosophers and psychologists, contemporary pastors: witnesses across centuries who have wrestled with the same human realities a given symbolic story explores. They provide wisdom, historical continuity, and practical insight. They do not validate the symbolic story or grant it Christian standing — an edition does not become faithful because a respected voice happens to agree with it. They witness that the reality it depicts is not novel or private; Christians have long contemplated it. **Voices from the Christian tradition should be selected because they illuminate the biblical witness rather than replace it. They serve Scripture, not the reverse.**
4. **The reader's lived experience** — where recognition becomes faithful living. This is the voice the first three exist to serve; none of them are complete until they meet a reader's own ordinary life.

Each voice does a different job, and none substitutes for another. Scripture is not merely illustrated by the symbolic story, nor is the symbolic story merely decorated with Scripture — the symbolic story prepares the imagination to receive what Scripture says, voices from the Christian tradition witness that the struggle and its resolution are not novel, and the reader is invited to recognize how all three converge within their own life.

**Every edition finds its own voices.** Which figures from the tradition illuminate a given edition is not fixed in advance and is not the same list reused across the series — it is discovered by researching that edition's own central struggle first, the way `Dragon_Christian_Tradition_Research.md` did for Dragon. A future edition's own research may turn up entirely different voices (Lewis and Nouwen again, perhaps, but also Madeleine L'Engle, Elisabeth Elliot, or a Church Father Dragon's research never needed) — richer than requiring every edition to sound alike by drawing on the same names.

See "The Larger Story" (Production Standard, Section 2) for how these four voices are built into a single page, and `Dragon_Christian_Tradition_Research.md` for the method this hierarchy was built to formalize.

### Christian Sentiment, Not Merely Christian Content

Recognition and Reflection are not becoming devotional pages, and this lens does not ask them to add Scripture, quotation, or explicit Christian language. It asks something more exacting: not "is Christian content present" but "is the sentiment faithful to the same understanding of the human person that The Larger Story makes explicit." A page can contain no Christian content at all and still fail this test, if its own implicit anthropology contradicts what The Larger Story later reveals — and a page can pass it while never naming Christ, Scripture, or grace, if what it says about the human person is quietly already true to them.

The test in practice: a line suggesting *"you already possess everything you need within yourself"* fails — not for lacking a citation, but for teaching a self-sufficient anthropology this lens has already ruled out (wholeness, per Recognition's own closing line, "is received before it is lived," not self-made). A line asking *"where do you recognize this struggle in your own life?"* passes — it invites recognition without implying that recognition is itself the resolution.

This is reviewed edition by edition, the way Scripture-selection already is — not a checklist applied once, but a question carried into every page: does this reflection prepare the reader for the Christian story, or does it quietly steer them toward a different understanding of the human person?

### The Six-Page Review Sequence

Each Figure Edition is now reviewed in this order, one page at a time, each against its own governing question rather than all six against one generic standard:

1. **Image** — Does it faithfully present the symbolic figure?
2. **Poem** — Does it awaken recognition?
3. **Recognition** — Does it name what is genuinely true?
4. **Reflection** — Does it invite honest engagement without prematurely resolving the struggle?
5. **The Larger Story** — Does it faithfully receive the edition into the Christian story?
6. **Living the Message** — Does it invite life in Christ rather than merely deeper self-awareness?

This sequence follows the edition's own actual movement rather than reviewing pages in isolation or out of order, and it is why Recognition and Reflection are now read for sentiment rather than edited for content — each page is doing a distinct job, and the review asks whether that job was done, not whether every page independently mentions Christ.

**Where this leaves The Larger Story.** It is treated as the one section of the architecture still seeking its mature voice — the newest addition, without years of implicit development behind it the way Recognition, Reflection, and Living the Message already have. Its eight revisions on Dragon are the record of that voice being found, not a sign that something is wrong with the page.

**The deeper discipline this points toward.** The working question is no longer "how do I make this edition Christian." It is "is every part of this edition faithful to the same understanding of reality." That is a harder, quieter standard than inserting Scripture where it fits — and it is the one meant to give AwakenArts a consistency readers feel without being able to name.

## The Governing Editorial Test

Every editorial decision — a line of copy, a page title, a new prompt, a proposed addition — should be measured against these five questions:

1. Does this deepen recognition?
2. Does this help the reader notice something previously unseen?
3. Does this preserve mystery rather than over-explain?
4. Does this encourage recognition in ordinary life?
5. Does this increase the usefulness of the edition?

If a proposed change does not pass, reconsider it before proceeding — regardless of how polished, clever, or complete it seems on its own terms.

---

## Governing Philosophy

An AwakenArts Figure Edition is successful when readers leave with a new way of seeing that continues to reveal itself within ordinary life.

Beautiful artwork invites attention. Language gives shape to experience. Recognition transforms the encounter. Ordinary life becomes the place where the edition continues.
