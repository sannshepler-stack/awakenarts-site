## Dove

- **Can suggest:** peace · Holy Spirit · remembrance · gentleness
- **Scripture:** Matthew 3:16 · Genesis 8:11 · Matthew 10:16
- **Note:** The dove appears in distinct biblical scenes, including Noah's account and Jesus' baptism.
- **Reflection question:** *Which associations with the dove are meaningful to you, and why?*
- **Related links:** none
- **Found by:** Dove, doves, white dove
- **Changes from your draft:** Added Matthew 10:16 ("innocent as doves") so "gentleness" has a supporting passage; Related: "water" → none (the link was to the setting of the scenes, not to what the dove suggests)
- **Scripture context:** Matthew 3:16: the Spirit descends "like a dove", a simile. Scripture does not say the Spirit is a dove.
- **Scripture context:** Genesis 8:11: the dove's olive leaf told Noah the waters had receded. It was a sign of new beginning; "peace" as a dove meaning is largely later and cultural.
- **Scripture context:** "Remembrance" is cultural, from doves released at memorials, not biblical. That suits a reference, but it is worth knowing.
- **Relation to published work:** A Journal entry, *The Dove*, is in progress. Once it is marked ready, search shows it and this reference steps aside automatically.

## Cross

- **Can suggest:** faith · sacrifice · redemption · hope · discipleship
- **Scripture:** 1 Corinthians 1:18 · Luke 9:23
- **Note:** For Christians, the cross is the place of Christ's death and the sign of redemption; many also wear or keep one as a personal sign of faith.
- **Reflection question:** *What experiences or commitments do you connect with the cross?*
- **Related links:** Path (`/symbols/path`)
- **Found by:** Cross, crosses, crucifix
- **Changes from your draft:** Note rewritten: names what the cross is in Christian faith, and its personal use, without denominational emphasis; "commitment" → "discipleship" (Luke 9:23, take up the cross and follow); added "redemption" (1 Corinthians 1:18)
- **Scripture context:** 1 Corinthians 1:18 and Luke 9:23 are central texts and are cited accurately.
- **Scripture context:** "Crucifix" stays as a search form because visitors will type it. The note speaks of the cross in a way all traditions share.

## Lily

- **Can suggest:** beauty · care · transience · hope
- **Scripture:** Matthew 6:28-30
- **Note:** Jesus points to lilies in a teaching about worry and provision.
- **Reflection question:** *What ordinary beauty invites you to pay attention?*
- **Related links:** Flower (`/journal?word=flower#symbol-vocabulary`)
- **Found by:** Lily, lilies, easter lily
- **Changes from your draft:** Removed "flower lily"; added "easter lily", a common search
- **Scripture context:** Matthew 6:28-30 is about God's care and freedom from worry ("consider the lilies"); verse 30's grass "thrown into the oven" supports "transience". The biblical flower was likely a wildflower, not the Easter lily, which is a later association.

- **Scripture:** Psalm 84:3 · Deuteronomy 32:11
- **Note:** A nest can evoke both shelter and the preparation to leave it.
- **Reflection question:** *What does the idea of a nest bring to mind about home?*
- **Related links:** Tree (`/journal?word=tree#symbol-vocabulary`)
- **Found by:** Nest, nests, bird nest, nesting, empty nest

- **Can suggest:** provision · abundance · calling · Christian identity
- **Scripture:** Matthew 14:17-21 · John 21:6 · Matthew 4:19
- **Note:** Fish appear in several Gospel narratives and in early Christian symbolism.
- **Reflection question:** *What associations do you bring to the image of a fish?*
- **Related links:** Net (`/symbols/net`)
- **Found by:** Fish, fishes, ichthys
- **Changes from your draft:** "faith; community" → "calling" (Matthew 4:19) and "Christian identity" (the ichthys sign); Added Matthew 4:19 ("fishers of men") for "calling"; Related: kept Net (Matthew 13:47, the net that gathers fish of every kind); removed Water

## Sheep

- **Can suggest:** belonging · being known · being sought · vulnerability · exclusion · individuality
- **Scripture:** John 10:11-16 · Luke 15:4-7
- **Note:** Scripture often speaks of God's people as a flock in a shepherd's care. The everyday phrase "black sheep" is not from Scripture; it names someone who stands apart from the family or group.
- **Reflection question:** *What does the image of a flock evoke about belonging?*
- **Related links:** Shepherd (`/symbols/shepherd`) · Gate (`/symbols/gate`)
- **Found by:** Sheep, flock, lost sheep, black sheep
- **Changes from your draft:** Added the familiar idioms "lost sheep" and "black sheep"; Added Luke 15:4-7 (the lost sheep); "community; care; trust" → "belonging; being known; being sought": the sheep's side of the Shepherd card
- **Scripture context:** John 10 and Luke 15 both speak from the sheep's side: known, sought, brought home. "Black sheep" is an everyday idiom, not biblical. The Lamb of God (John 1:29) is kept out deliberately, as a distinct image.
- **Relation to published work:** Distinct from the published **Shepherd** Symbol Card (care, guidance, being known): this reference speaks from the sheep's side and links to that card.

## Olive Branch

- **Can suggest:** peace · hope · restoration · reconciliation
- **Scripture:** Genesis 8:11
- **Note:** The olive leaf appears in the account of Noah's dove.
- **Reflection question:** *Is there a relationship where an olive branch might be offered or received?*
- **Related links:** none
- **Found by:** Olive Branch, olive branches, olive leaf
- **Changes from your draft:** Question reworded so it does not assume a strained relationship
- **Scripture context:** Genesis 8:11 says "olive leaf". "Extending an olive branch" as a gesture of peace comes mostly from later Greek and Roman use, and the note keeps to the Genesis scene.
